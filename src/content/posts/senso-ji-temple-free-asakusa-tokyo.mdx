Short answer, since it's the one people actually search for: **yes, Senso-ji is free.** No entry fee, no ticket booth, no timed-entry app. You walk in through the Kaminarimon gate, you walk out whenever you want, and it costs you nothing. It's Tokyo's oldest temple, it's lit up every night, and it is, hands down, one of the best free things to do anywhere in the city.

We've been back to Asakusa more than once now, and this last time we went at night with all three kids — Lily, Cora, and Harper — specifically because that's when it's at its best: fewer crowds, the pagoda lit orange-red against the sky, and Tokyo Skytree glowing behind it.

## What's actually free here

- **Walking the temple grounds and the pagoda** — open 24 hours, no fee, ever.
- **Kaminarimon gate and the giant lantern** — the single most photographed spot in Asakusa, and it costs nothing to stand under it.
- **Nakamise-dori**, the shopping street leading up to the temple — browsing is free, and half the fun is just looking at the stalls even if you don't buy anything.
- **Senso-ji's main hall at night**, lit up after the daytime crowds thin out.

The only things you'll spend money on are optional: a fortune slip (omikuji) is usually around ¥100, and if you want a souvenir or a snack off Nakamise-dori, that's on you.

## Where the actual money goes: dinner

Free temple, but everyone still has to eat, and this is where Asakusa quietly saves you money if you know where to look. We ended up at **Torikizoku**, a nationwide yakitori and izakaya chain where almost everything on the menu — skewers, drinks, sides — is priced flat, usually right around ¥300 per item (a couple bucks). It's the kind of place where a family of five can eat and drink well for what one nice dinner out would cost back home.

If you want to splurge instead, Asakusa also has excellent yakiniku — we did a grill-it-yourself wagyu night a couple days earlier for a very different price point. Point is, the neighborhood flexes both directions; the temple visit itself is what's free, not the whole night.

## What an evening in Asakusa actually costs (per person, rough USD)

| Thing | Cost |
|---|---|
| Senso-ji temple + Nakamise-dori browsing | $0 |
| Omikuji fortune slip (optional) | ~$0.70 |
| Street snack off Nakamise-dori (optional) | $2–5 |
| Dinner at a yakitori chain like Torikizoku | $15–25 |
| Splurge yakiniku dinner instead | $35–60 |

So a full evening — temple, walking the shopping street, and a real dinner — lands somewhere around $20/person if you keep it casual, before you've spent a cent on "things to do."
