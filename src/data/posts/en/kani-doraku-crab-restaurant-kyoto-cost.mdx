Dinner for the five of us at Kani Doraku in Kyoto came to 27,000 yen, about $192 at the exchange rate that week. That was a lot more than we expected to spend, and by the end of the night we agreed we would rather have put the same money toward a good steak dinner. The kids had a great time. The adults learned a few things the expensive way, and they're the reason this post exists.

## The day that led to dinner

This was a planned dinner, not an impulse stop. We left that morning saying we'd walk Gion, the old geisha district, and finish at "that Kani Doraku crab place" for dinner. It was late November 2022, Japan had only just reopened to tourists, and the leaves were turning. We crossed the bridge into Gion in perfect weather, then walked through Nishiki Market, a covered street of meat, produce and spice stalls that was really crowded. We were glad Harper was riding on one of our backs.

Kani Doraku is the chain behind the giant moving crab signs. The first went up over the original restaurant on Dotonbori in Osaka, which opened in 1960, and you can see it on a [free night walk along Dotonbori](/blog/dotonbori-free-night-walk-osaka/).

## What we ordered, and what it cost

We were shown to a private room with tatami seating, shoes off at the door. It was the first restaurant in Japan where we'd had to take our shoes off, and the kids loved it. Then came the menu: set courses built on different crabs, crab sushi, raw crab, king crab, crab soup, crab rice, and pages of a la carte. We ordered "a bunch of stuff," as we put it on camera, and by the time the food arrived we couldn't remember all of it.

Here's what reached the table:

| Dish | How it went |
|---|---|
| Raw crab sashimi | Our verdict on camera: like swallowing a slug. Never again. |
| Boiled crab with vinegar dip | Good, but different from the melted butter we use at home |
| Grilled snow crab legs | Good, and the dish we'd order again |
| Kani-suki crab hot pot | Tasty, but we didn't know the steps (more below) |
| Crab tempura | Looked amazing on arrival |
| Crab rice in a clay pot | Really good |
| Ice cream | To finish |
| **Total for five** | **27,000 yen, about $192 then** |

We don't have the itemized receipt, only the total we said on camera when we walked out. For comparison, the Kyoto branch's current published set courses are all nine dishes and run 4,730 yen (Hinata), 5,280 yen (Misaki), 5,720 yen (Koharu), 6,050 yen (Nagisa) and 7,260 yen (Yuzuki) per person, tax included. Five of the cheapest course comes to 23,650 yen, and five of the middle Koharu course to 28,600 yen. Our a la carte order cost about the same as putting the whole family on a mid-tier course, without the structure a course gives you. Prices in 2022 may have differed from today's menu.

## The hot pot is a cooking lesson

The kani-suki hot pot comes raw, with instructions: wait for the broth to steam, add the vegetables, add the crab, cook about two minutes, then add one or two drops of the white soy sauce, which the card promises will make it "much more delicious." You also get a special crab tool for digging meat out of the shell, alongside regular chopsticks.

None of that is hard, but we didn't know the steps, and as we said on camera that night, it made dinner stressful and we only enjoyed about half of the meal. If you've never eaten a Japanese crab course before, it helps to go with someone who has, or to read up on kani-suki before you sit down.

## How to spend less than we did

- **Order the grilled crab legs a la carte.** That was our own advice on camera at the end of the night, and it skips the dishes you may not like.
- **Skip the raw crab unless you know you like it.** Check whether a set course includes crab sashimi before you order it.
- **Or pick one set course and stick to it.** If you want the full experience, a fixed course at least tells you the price before you start.
- **Book ahead for a group.** Kani Doraku is a well-known chain, and a table for five at dinner time is easier to get with a reservation through the restaurant's own site or your hotel.

## Was it worth it

For the kids, yes. Harper and Lily loved the crab, the girls behaved well, and a private tatami room with shoes off was a new experience for all of us. For the two of us paying, it was a one-time thing. Crab in this style isn't really our food, and 27,000 yen at a steakhouse would have made us happier. That's the honest takeaway: if your family already loves crab, Kani Doraku is a fun Kyoto splurge. If you're only going because the giant crab sign looks cool, admire the sign, take the photo, and spend your yen on dinner somewhere else.
