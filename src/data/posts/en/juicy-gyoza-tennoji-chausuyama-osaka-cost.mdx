Not every restaurant we've written up came with a name we could read at the table. This one did: a coaster reading "Quality Brand, The Juicy Gyoza Manufactory" in English, sitting on the table in Chausuyama, a small hill and park area inside Tennoji, Osaka. That English branding is a direct translation of 肉汁餃子のダンダダン, Nikujiru Gyoza no Dandadan, "juicy gyoza Dandadan," a real gyoza chain with a Tennoji location that matches exactly.

## The neighborhood the name comes from

Chausuyama isn't a made-up restaurant-district name, it's a real hill inside Tennoji Park with a genuine place in Japanese history. On June 3 to 4, 1615, the samurai Sanada Yukimura took position on Chausuyama during the Battle of Tennōji, the final engagement of the Siege of Osaka, the campaign that ended the Toyotomi clan's last armed resistance to the Tokugawa shogunate and settled who ruled Japan for the next two and a half centuries. The Toyotomi side lost more than 15,000 men in that fight. None of that history is on the menu, but it's a short walk from a gyoza counter today to a monument marking one of the more consequential battles in the country's history.

## The cheap order

Dandadan's signature dish, the one the chain is built around, is grilled juicy gyoza, six pieces for 616 yen. Pair that with chilled tofu or edamame, 429 to 528 yen, and a single round of gyoza plus a starter clears 1,000 yen without trying. The restaurant's own stated average budget for a meal here is 1,500 yen, which tracks: one order of the signature dish and a side dish is most of what a quick, cheap stop here looks like.

## The splurge order

Keep going and the same table adds up fast. Dandadan also serves fresh horse sashimi, basashi, a real regional specialty rather than a novelty item, running 1,078 to 1,518 yen depending on the cut. Add a second order of gyoza, a round of drinks, and one or two more shared plates, and the restaurant's own stated dinner average climbs to 3,500 yen, more than double the lunch figure for what's still fundamentally the same gyoza-first menu.

| | Cheap order | Splurge order |
|---|---|---|
| Gyoza | 616 yen (6 pieces) | 616 yen x 2 rounds |
| Side | Tofu or edamame, 429-528 yen | Basashi, 1,078-1,518 yen |
| Typical total | ~1,500 yen | ~3,500 yen |

## Was it worth it

We don't have a full receipt from that specific night, so we won't invent an exact total for our table, but the real published numbers make the pattern clear either way: a gyoza chain built around one cheap, well-reviewed signature dish works at both ends of the budget. Order light and it's one of the cheapest sit-down dinners in Osaka. Add the basashi and a second round, and it's still a real meal out, not a splurge in the Sakai-teppanyaki sense, just a fuller one. Either way beats guessing at a menu with no English on it, which is most of what a coaster photo and a little research can fix after the fact.
