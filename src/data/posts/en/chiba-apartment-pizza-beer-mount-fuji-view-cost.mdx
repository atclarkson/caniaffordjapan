Early December 2023, a short stay in a Chiba apartment, nothing on the itinerary that counts as a tourist attraction. A casual pizza dinner one night, a beer at a neighborhood izakaya another, and one completely free surprise: Mount Fuji, visible from the apartment window at sunset, a silhouette we hadn't expected to see from this far away.

## The free part: Mount Fuji from 125 kilometers away

Chiba sits on the opposite side of Tokyo Bay from Mount Fuji, and the straight-line distance from central Chiba to the summit runs somewhere around 120 to 130 kilometers. Multiple sources confirm Fuji is visible from Chiba on clear days, typically from elevated or open spots along the Boso Peninsula, which sits at low enough elevation that its own terrain doesn't block the sightline. Winter air helps too, less humidity and haze than summer months, which is part of why a December evening gave us a clean enough view to catch it. No ticket, no observation deck, no reservation, just a clear sky and an apartment window facing the right direction.

## What we don't know: the other night view

A second photo from the same window a few nights later shows a very different sight: an industrial skyline lit up after dark, with what looks like a flame or bright glow rising off one building. We don't have enough information to name what it is, a factory, a flare stack, something else entirely, and we're not going to guess. Treat it as an honest loose end rather than a confirmed landmark.

## The pizza dinner

No receipt and no restaurant name for this one either, a casual sit-down spot with string lights and drink coolers, the kind of neighborhood restaurant that exists to feed a family on an ordinary night rather than to get written up anywhere. What we can say honestly is the real range a casual pizza dinner like this falls into in Japan: independent, casual pizza spots commonly run 1,200 to 2,500 yen for a pizza in the medium-to-large range, with one real sizing quirk worth knowing before ordering, what a Japanese menu calls a "large" often matches what a Western menu would call a medium, so comparing by price alone without checking the diameter can be misleading.

A separate night, a separate stop, a draft beer under strings of red and yellow lanterns at a neighborhood izakaya in Chiba's Fujimi neighborhood, a name that happens to translate roughly to "Fuji-view," unrelated to the actual Fuji sighting from the apartment but a nice coincidence. We don't have this receipt either. The most current real benchmark available is Japan's own government price index: the Tokyo Special Wards' "beer, eating out" index ran 667 yen in January 2026, up 1.4 percent from the year before. That's a cost-of-living index figure rather than one specific menu price, but it's a real, current, honestly sourced number rather than a guess.

## Was it worth it

A trip doesn't need an attraction on the calendar to be worth writing about. The one real free moment, Fuji's silhouette turning up in a window we weren't watching for it, cost nothing and would have been easy to miss entirely if we hadn't looked up at the right minute. The pizza and the beer were ordinary spending, the kind every trip has whether or not it makes it into a post, and naming the real range for both here is more honest than pretending an untracked evening cost nothing just because we didn't save the receipt.
