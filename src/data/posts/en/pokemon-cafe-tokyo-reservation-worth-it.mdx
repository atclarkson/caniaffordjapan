We visited the Pokémon Café in Nihonbashi while my parents were in town, including my dad's 70th birthday, a real reason to pick a splurge instead of our usual budget-first approach. Getting the reservation itself was its own small ordeal, which is worth knowing before you plan around this one.

## Where it is

The café sits on the 5th floor of Nihombashi Takashimaya's East Building, a 4-minute walk from Nihombashi Station and about 10 minutes from Tokyo Station's Yaesu exit. It's a single, real location, not a chain, which is part of why booking a table is harder than almost anything else food-related in this city.

## The booking system, which is the real story here

There are effectively no walk-ins. Every seat goes through the official Pokémon Café website, and the system is a real test of patience: reservations for a given date open exactly 31 days ahead at 6:00 PM Japan time and close at 8:00 AM the day of your visit, but in practice the real window is the first few seconds after 6:00 PM, when the site frequently crashes under the load and slots sell out before most people can finish loading the page. Online cancellation stays free until 10:00 PM the night before; after that you have to call to cancel.

The café was also closed for renovation from March 23 to June 16, 2026, reopening with reservations for the new period released May 17, 2026 at 6:00 PM, the same high-pressure format as before. If you're planning a visit now, in October, the system has settled back into its normal 31-day rolling window, so the practical advice is the same as it's always been: pick your date, set an alarm for 6:00 PM Japan time exactly 31 days out, and refresh.

We found no confirmed Klook or Viator listing for a guaranteed table as of this writing, and third-party resale options we came across charge a separate, non-refundable service fee just for a reservation slot, on top of whatever you spend inside. Treat the official site as the only real path in rather than paying extra for a workaround that may not even be legitimate.

## What a real meal costs

Reported prices vary enough between sources that we're giving you a range rather than a single confident number, menu pricing shifted with the 2026 reopening and not every listing has caught up. Character-themed plates have run somewhere around 1,700 to 2,400 yen each in recent reporting (a Pikachu plate, a Snorlax lunch plate), with themed drinks running roughly 660 to 1,760 yen. Put a full meal together and most visitor estimates land in the 2,500 to 4,000 yen per person range, food and drink included. No confirmed cover charge on top of that as of this writing, though menu items themselves do the work of making the bill add up the way a cover charge would elsewhere.

## What you're paying for

The food itself gets mixed reviews across the sources we checked, this is a themed experience built around presentation and character design more than a kitchen trying to impress you on flavor alone. What it does deliver is real: Pikachu and other character mascots occasionally make appearances table-side, the plating is elaborate for what's ultimately simple cafe food, and the gift shop sells merchandise you can't get anywhere else, including outside Japan. For a family built around a real occasion, a birthday, a last day in Tokyo, a kid who's been asking for this specific place by name, that combination is the actual product, not the calories.

## A real history, not just a theme

The Pokémon Café opened at this exact location on March 14, 2018, the first permanent official Pokémon café anywhere, and drew nearly 100,000 visitors in its first six months alone, a real sign the booking crush we described above isn't a recent development. A second location opened in Shinsaibashi, Osaka, in September 2019. This isn't a pop-up or a limited-run marketing stunt, it's a seven-plus-year-old permanent restaurant that has stayed this hard to book the entire time.

## What a reserved seat gets you

The café seats 140 diners at once across communal wooden tables, no private rooms or booths, so a larger party should expect to sit near, not necessarily beside, other diners. Each reservation comes with a hard 90-minute time limit, and the clock runs on your booked time slot no matter when you arrive, the café's own advice is to show up about 10 minutes early rather than cutting it close. Arrive more than 15 minutes late and the table can be given up entirely. Sources disagree on the maximum group size, somewhere between 6 and 8 people depending on which guide you check, and at least one notes that once you've booked, you can't change the party size afterward, so count your group carefully before you commit to a slot.

## The merchandise angle

This is the part that's easy to undersell: the café shop sells goods you can't get anywhere else, not at a regular Pokémon Center, not online. Chef Pikachu plushies come in several color variants (pink, mint, blue, black, red, white reported across different stock runs), alongside Pokeball-shaped mugs and cups and branded thermos flasks. We've already covered the real pricing at the nearby Pokémon Center Tokyo DX in Nihonbashi separately, that store stocks its own exclusives, including regional-themed Pikachu designs, but the café's own merchandise line is a distinct, separate set of items tied specifically to this location. Stock rotates with the seasons and some items go pre-order only, so don't plan a trip around one specific design without checking current availability first.

## Is it worth the hassle

Yes, with a real condition attached: it's worth it if you treat the reservation itself as part of the experience rather than an annoying gatekeeper, and if the exclusive merchandise and the theming are the real draw for your family rather than expecting a meal that would stand on its own without the branding. It's not worth it if you're trying to squeeze it into a tight itinerary on short notice, the booking system doesn't accommodate spontaneity, and it's not worth chasing if your group would be just as happy at the Pokémon Center next door without a seat reservation attached. For an occasion worth building a day around, which is exactly how we used it, the answer holds up.

## Getting there

Nihombashi Takashimaya sits directly on the Tokyo Metro Ginza and Tozai lines at Nihombashi Station, with Tokyo Station itself an easy walk via the Yaesu side, making this one of the more centrally located splurges on this site rather than a dedicated day-trip destination on its own.
