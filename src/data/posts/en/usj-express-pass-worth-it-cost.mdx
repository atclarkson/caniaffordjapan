April 2026 was our fourth trip through Universal Studios Japan's gates, enough visits that we've stopped debating whether the park itself is worth the ticket and started debating something more specific: whether the Express Pass is worth the extra cost on top of it. We already broke down base ticket pricing for USJ against Tokyo Disney in an earlier post. This one is about the add-on almost everyone weighs once they're actually booking.

## What the Express Pass actually is

It's easy to assume "Express Pass" is a ticket type. It isn't, it's a separate product you buy in addition to a regular 1-Day Studio Pass, not instead of one. Buying only an Express Pass with no park admission doesn't get you through the gate at all.

## What it costs

| Pass | Attractions covered | Price range |
|---|---|---|
| Express Pass 4 | 6 to 7 rides in practice | 9,500 to 20,000 yen |
| Express Pass 7 | 9 to 10 rides in practice | 11,500 to 26,000 yen |

Like the base ticket, Express Pass pricing moves with the calendar, weekends, holidays, and peak season push both tiers toward the higher end of those ranges. The exact ride list depends on which specific Express Pass variant you pick, not every option includes Super Nintendo World access, so read the fine print on the version you're buying rather than assuming all Express 4 passes are identical. Popular passes covering rides like Harry Potter and the Forbidden Journey, Mario Kart: Koopa's Challenge, or Despicable Me Minion Mayhem tend to sell out first, sometimes within hours once a date's calendar opens, so this isn't a decision you can put off until the morning of your visit if you want a specific pass.

## The math worth doing before you buy

An Express Pass only pays for itself in time, not money, so the real question is what an hour of standing in line is worth to your family that day. If a headline ride like the Harry Potter attraction is running a 90 minute wait and the Express Pass cuts that to 10, you're buying back roughly 80 minutes per ride covered. Multiply that across even four or five rides on a busy day and the math tilts toward the pass being worth it specifically on high-crowd days, weekends, school holidays, the exact days when the pass itself also costs the most. On a genuinely quiet weekday, standby lines can already run short enough that paying extra buys back very little, which is the opposite side of the same seasonal pricing.

## Free ways to cut the wait anyway

Before paying for an Express Pass at all, it's worth knowing USJ has a few genuinely free ways to shorten the day without spending anything extra. Several of the park's more popular rides run a Single Rider line, you'll be split from your group and ride alone or paired with strangers, but it can cut a long standby wait meaningfully on the right ride, JAWS in particular is the one most guides point to as worth using. Universal Wonderland attractions use a separate Reserve and Ride system tied to your Studio Pass rather than an Express Pass, and both the Wizarding World of Harry Potter and Super Nintendo World areas hand out timed-entry tickets at Central Park that don't cost anything beyond admission either. None of these fully replace what an Express Pass buys, but they're worth working into the day before deciding the extra cost is necessary.

## Minion Park itself

The area behind that Despicable Me photo wall in our own pictures, Minion Park, opened back in April 2017 and has kept expanding since, most recently with a new Villain-Con Minion Blast interactive attraction added in 2025. It's one of the park's few zones you can genuinely enjoy without an Express Pass mattering much, the photo ops and walkthrough areas don't have the same headline-ride wait times as Harry Potter or Super Nintendo World, making it a reasonable stop to fill time on the same day you're using a pass elsewhere.

## Was it worth it

We don't have a single answer, because the honest answer changes by trip. A family visiting once, on a packed Golden Week or summer weekend, gets real value back in saved time. A family who can pick a quiet Tuesday in the off season may get more value just pocketing the extra yen and accepting a normal wait. Four visits in, that's the actual lesson: the Express Pass isn't a universal upgrade, it's a bet on the specific day you've booked, and it's worth checking that day's crowd calendar before you decide rather than buying it on reflex every time.
