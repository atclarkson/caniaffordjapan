Free, no ticket, no booth, just a 20-meter robot standing in an open plaza you could walk up to any time of day. That was the Unicorn Gundam at DiverCity Tokyo Plaza in Odaiba, on display from September 2017 until it came down for good on August 31, 2026. We photographed it in November 2023, a different visit from the Odaiba day trip we've already written about, before the removal date was ever announced.

## What it was

Modeled on the robot from the anime series Mobile Suit Gundam Unicorn, the statue stood about 20 meters tall (close to 65 feet) and weighed 49 tons, built life-size to the scale the anime itself uses. It replaced an earlier RX-78-2 Gundam statue that had stood on the same spot since 2012 (after an earlier, separate stint at Shiokaze Park in 2009 and a stop in Shizuoka in between) and came down in spring 2017, making way for this one a few months later. Scheduled shows switched the statue between two modes, Unicorn and Destroy, the head physically reconfiguring and panels across the body lighting up red in sync with music, a free performance that ran on its own schedule throughout the day.

## What it cost to see

Nothing. It stood in DiverCity's open outdoor Festival Plaza, not inside a ticketed zone, with nothing we found suggesting an admission charge for walking up and photographing it, same as the Doraemon Time Square statues a floor up inside the building. The Gundam Base Tokyo store nearby does sell real money on Gundam model kits and merchandise, that's a separate retail stop, not a cost of seeing the statue itself.

## The last day

The statue's final run ended on a Monday, August 31, 2026, with crowds turning out for a last look before it came down, according to Japanese press coverage of the farewell. No word yet on where it went after dismantling, the same uncertainty that followed the earlier RX-78-2 statue when it left Odaiba in 2017, its next stop wasn't confirmed for a while either. A Gundam statue leaving DiverCity apparently isn't a one-time event, just one that happens on a roughly decade-long cycle.

## Why this matters if you're planning a trip

We already wrote honestly about this in our Odaiba day trip post: if a Gundam photo is the reason you're building a day around DiverCity, that reason doesn't hold anymore. Worth repeating here with the real photo evidence rather than secondhand reporting. DiverCity Tokyo Plaza itself is still there, Doraemon Time Square and the Hello Kitty Japan store are both still free and still open, Round1's arcade floor is still upstairs. The one specific landmark that drew people to that exact plaza for almost nine years is simply gone, and nothing announced yet says what replaces it. We've already covered how to get to DiverCity, the water bus from Asakusa included, in that Odaiba post, no reason to repeat the same transit details here.

## Was it worth seeing

For us, yes, we just didn't know at the time we were looking at something with a countdown on it. If you're reading this before a Japan trip and DiverCity is already on the itinerary for other reasons, Doraemon and Hello Kitty, the arcades, the water bus over from Asakusa, it's still a full free day. Just don't build the day around a statue that isn't standing there anymore.
