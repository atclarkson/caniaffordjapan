We already broke down what a Tokyo Disneyland or DisneySea ticket costs in an earlier post. What we didn't cover is everything that happens after you're through the gate, because a theme park day is never just the admission price. We did Disneyland one day and DisneySea the next in September 2025, and kept enough of the real numbers in our heads to write this one honestly.

## Getting to Urayasu

Tokyo Disney Resort isn't actually in Tokyo, it's in Urayasu, Chiba Prefecture, just across the city line. The JR Keiyo Line runs straight there from Tokyo Station to Maihama Station, about 20 minutes for roughly 230 yen one way, easy to fold into a normal Suica or Pasmo tap. From Maihama, the resort's own monorail, the Disney Resort Line, loops between the station and both parks plus the resort hotels.

A single Disney Resort Line ride is 300 yen for adults, 150 yen for kids, but a one-day pass runs 700 yen and pays for itself the moment you ride it more than twice, which we did both days moving between the station, the park, and back again at night. Direct highway buses also run to the resort from Tokyo Station, Shinjuku, and Narita Airport if you'd rather skip the train transfer entirely, though we stuck with the JR line both days since our hotel was already close to a Keiyo Line stop.

## What actually adds up once you're inside

Snacks are where a Disney day quietly gets expensive, five people eating small treats throughout the day turns into real money fast. The Mickey Ice Bar, the tropical fruit flavored, Mickey-head-shaped ice cream bar that shows up in half our photos, runs 400 yen. For the five of us to each get one, that's 2,000 yen in ice cream alone, and we did it more than once across the two days.

Popcorn is its own category entirely. A plain box with no bucket is 400 yen, a refill in a bucket you already own is 600 yen, and the hard plastic souvenir buckets themselves, the ones shaped like whatever character or attraction is trending that season, run 2,400 to 3,200 yen depending on the design. We didn't buy one on this trip, but they're everywhere in the park and easy to see why people do, they're genuinely well made and double as a keepsake rather than something that gets thrown out that night.

Small plush souvenirs are the other place money goes without much planning. Cora and Harper each picked one up on the DisneySea day, and by the time we were on the monorail back to Maihama, the new plushies had their own seat.

## What the two days actually cost beyond the tickets

Add it up for a family our size and the pattern is clear: transit in and around the resort came to a few hundred yen per person each way, one round of Mickey ice cream bars for everyone was 2,000 yen, and that's before popcorn, a second snack round, or anything with a character's face on it that someone wanted to take home. None of it is unreasonable on its own, a 400 yen ice cream bar is not a splurge, but five people doing it more than once a day across two days adds up to real money that the ticket price alone doesn't tell you about.

## Was it worth it

Completely, and not because of any one souvenir or snack. Two days, two very different parks, a monorail the kids treated like its own ride, and enough Mickey-shaped ice cream to remember exactly what it tasted like a year later. Budget past the ticket price going in, and none of the extra spending feels like it caught you off guard once you're actually there.
