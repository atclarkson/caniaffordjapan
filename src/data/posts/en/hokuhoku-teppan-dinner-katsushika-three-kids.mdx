Not every meal in Japan needs to be a landmark. Most of an extended stay in Katsushika was just this: a neighborhood teppan restaurant a short walk from the apartment, kids' cups on the table, nothing about the evening planned more than a few minutes in advance. The glass gave the name away, HOKU HOKU printed right on it, and that was enough to track down exactly where we'd been and what it costs today.

## Finding HOKUHOKU

HOKUHOKU Teppan Dining sits about a minute's walk from Keisei Takasago Station in Katsushika, a real, current, sit-down teppanyaki spot rather than a tourist-facing chain. It's closed Tuesdays, and the kind of place that exists for the neighborhood around it rather than for visitors passing through, which is exactly why it never would have turned up on a guidebook list.

## What it costs

HOKUHOKU's current published menu confirms real pricing for several of the items on our table that night: garlic rice (the sizzling plate above, made with garlic, carrot, green pepper, onion, and pork) runs 650 yen, a house salad is 500 yen, chashu is 750 yen, a mixed okonomiyaki is 1,850 yen, and sauce yakisoba is 650 yen. The restaurant's own stated average budget per person is around 3,000 yen, squarely in casual-dinner territory rather than special-occasion pricing.

We don't have the full receipt from that specific night, so we won't invent a family total, but with real 650 to 1,850 yen dishes on the menu and a stated 3,000 yen average per adult, a casual dinner for five here lands well under what a single teppanyaki course costs at the high-end Sakai restaurant we've written about elsewhere on this site. Same cooking style, a completely different price category.

## Doing it with three kids

Nothing about HOKUHOKU is set up for tourists, no English menu photos to point at, no kids' play area, just a neighborhood restaurant with teppan tables and regular customers. That's exactly what made it easy: no crowd, no wait, and a staff used to a full table rather than a reservation system built around turning tables quickly. The girls had their own cups, picked at the garlic rice, and the French toast skillet did the job dessert always does at the end of a long day. None of it required planning ahead the way a destination restaurant would, which after enough days of timed entries and reservations is its own kind of relief.

## Getting there

HOKUHOKU is about a one minute walk from Keisei Takasago Station, on the Keisei main line, a station most visitors never have a reason to stop at since it isn't near any major attraction. That's the real pattern worth noting: a long stay in one neighborhood means a lot of evenings happen within a five minute walk of wherever you're sleeping, not at a destination you traveled across the city for. HOKUHOKU wasn't picked from a list, it was picked because it was close, open, and had a free table, the same way most of our actual weeknight dinners in Japan got chosen.

## Was it worth it

Completely, and for a reason that has nothing to do with the food being extraordinary. It was ordinary, in the best sense, a real local spot that happened to be close to where we were staying, with prices that let a family dinner stay a family dinner rather than an event. If a glass or a menu gives you a name to search, it's worth doing, sometimes the most useful post-trip discovery is just confirming that the quiet neighborhood night out was as reasonably priced as it felt at the table.
