Most of what we post here is about spending less. This one is the opposite. For Lindsay's 40th birthday in February 2026, we sat down at a teppanyaki counter in Sakai, just outside Osaka, and the bill came to more than $300 for the meal. It's still the most expensive dinner we've ever had as a family, and neither of us regrets it.

## What a normal night there actually costs

The restaurant is Nankai Grill, right by the east exit of Sakai Station on the Nankai Main Line. Their own posted pricing lists a dinner course averaging around 7,500 yen a person (about $48), lunch closer to 4,000 yen (about $26), built around Miyazaki wagyu with a salad bar and soft drinks included. That's a real splurge by our usual standards, but nowhere near $300.

We didn't order the standard course. We added lobster and extra wagyu on top of it, plus drinks, and that's what moved the number from "nice dinner out" to "most expensive meal of our lives."

## Where the money actually went

- **The base course.** Even the standard dinner course at 7,500 yen a person is already a step up from most of what we eat on a normal night in Japan, which usually runs us under $10 a person.
- **Lobster, added on.** Not part of the regular course. Ordered separately, cooked right on the teppan next to the wagyu.
- **Extra wagyu.** Beyond what the base course includes, the good cuts aren't cheap anywhere in Japan and this was no exception.
- **Drinks.** A birthday dinner without a couple of drinks isn't really a birthday dinner.

## What the day looked like around it

We didn't build the whole day around an expensive dinner. Earlier that afternoon the five of us went bowling at a Round1 near Sakai Station, the kind of loud, cheap, air conditioned family activity that fills most of our actual days in Japan. Adam won, 119 to 100. Then the girls came along to see the restaurant from outside, and the birthday dinner itself was just the two of us at the counter.

That's closer to how we actually travel than the $300 receipt suggests. One splurge dinner doesn't erase a trip built on free temples and convenience store lunches, it just means once in a while, on a birthday that only happens every ten years, we let the budget go.

## Is it worth it

If you're weighing a splurge dinner like this on your own trip: yes, if it's for something that matters to you, and no, if you're doing it because you feel like you're supposed to try teppanyaki once. The 7,500 yen standard course gets you real wagyu and a real teppanyaki show without the add ons that got us to $300. Save the extras for the birthday.
