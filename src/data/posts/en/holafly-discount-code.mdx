## Two different products, two different discounts

Holafly sells two things, and the code does something different on each one:

- **Destination eSIMs.** A one-off eSIM for a single country or region, unlimited data for a set number of days, bought once per trip. The code takes **5% off**.
- **Holafly Plans.** A subscription eSIM that works across 160+ destinations on one install, billed monthly, quarterly, or annually. The code takes **10% off**. Plans comes in three tiers: Light (25GB), Unlimited (unlimited data), and a newer Ultra tier (unlimited data plus a bundle of travel support features, launched October 2026).

A short trip to one country is almost always the destination eSIM, a traveler bouncing between several countries over months is the one Plans is built for.

It's data only, no phone number, so calls and texts route through an app like WhatsApp rather than a local number. Each plan also includes 1GB of "Always On" backup data a month across 150+ countries once the eSIM stays installed, and hotspot sharing up to 1GB a day. ## What Holafly Plans costs

For a traveler who's not doing a single Japan trip but moving between countries over weeks or months, the subscription avoids buying a new eSIM every time:

| Tier | Data | What's different |
|---|---|---|
| Light | 25GB | The entry tier |
| Unlimited | Unlimited | Adds a local number for inbound SMS |
| Ultra | Unlimited | Adds 24/7 doctor chat, flight and luggage claim handling, and a handful of wellness add-ons (psychologist, nutritionist, personal trainer sessions) |

Quarterly and annual billing both come at a discount over paying monthly, Holafly's own Plans page lists roughly 10% off quarterly and 20% off annual, though it doesn't publish the resulting per-month price outright, so the exact number is worth confirming at checkout rather than assuming. Ultra is a real step up in price for what's mostly a short-trip audience, a family doing two weeks in Japan is better served by the destination eSIM above than a subscription built for someone living out of a suitcase for months.

## Was it worth it

Yes, for the one thing it solves: not hunting for a SIM card counter at the airport or figuring out a foreign carrier's app at 11pm after a long flight. We install it before we fly, it's working the moment we land, and the 5% knocks a real few dollars off a cost we were paying anyway. It's not the cheapest eSIM on the market dollar for dollar, Airalo runs slightly less, but the code closes most of that gap, and we'd rather use the one we've relied on ourselves than chase the lowest sticker price on a new provider every trip.
