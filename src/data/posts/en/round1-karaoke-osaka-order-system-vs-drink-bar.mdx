This is a different Round1 trip than [the one on Lindsay's birthday](/blog/round1-bowling-arcade-cost-japan-family/) or [the bowling-shoe vending machine one for Harper's](/blog/round1-bowling-shoe-vending-machine-harper-birthday/), both from the same stretch of 2023. This visit was just the two of us, no bowling lane this time, straight to the karaoke floor for a parents-only evening out.

## What a karaoke room in Japan is

For anyone picturing karaoke as a bar with a stage and a stranger's name called off a sign-up sheet, Japanese karaoke isn't that. It's a private room, booked by the hour or half-hour, just the people in your group, a screen, a stack of remotes, and a phone on the wall to call in a drink order. Nobody outside the room hears you unless the walls are thin, and nobody's waiting their turn to get on a mic, every room runs a session on a separate schedule. That's the format Round1's karaoke floor runs on too, the same private-room model as a dedicated chain, just folded into a building that also has a bowling alley and an arcade one floor over.

## Karaoke at Round1 doesn't price like a dedicated karaoke chain

A standalone karaoke chain in Japan, Big Echo or Karaoke Kan, usually quotes a clean number: so many yen per 30 minutes per person, done. Round1's pricing page doesn't work that way. Karaoke sits inside the same entertainment-complex structure as the bowling and arcade floors, and the base time-block rates are published as image graphics on the store's page rather than plain text, varying by store, by day of the week, and by time slot, not one number that holds everywhere. What the page does say in plain text is the part that changes how a visit gets billed: a one-order-per-person system, "お1人様につき1オーダー制440円〜," at least one food or drink order per person starting from 440 yen, the drink bar itself counting as that order.

This isn't a Round1-only quirk. The same one-order-per-person rule (一人一品制, "one item per person") shows up across Japanese izakayas and bars generally, often alongside a small flat seating charge (otoshi) that isn't optional either. A room or a table isn't free to occupy without ordering something, karaoke at Round1 just applies the same logic to a room with a microphone in it instead of a dinner table.

## The cheap version: just the minimum order

The floor of what a karaoke room costs at Round1 isn't the room time, it's the order minimum. One drink-bar order per person at 440 yen and up covers the requirement, no separate per-head cover charge stacked on top of it as far as the store's page states. For two people stopping in for an hour between other plans, that's the lean version: pay for the room time itself (whatever the store's current block rate happens to be that day), plus one drink each to clear the order minimum, and that's the whole bill.

## The splurge version: all-you-can-drink

The other end of it is a nomihoudai (all-you-can-drink) add-on, standard across most Japanese karaoke venues including Round1's group-plan options, typically another 1,000 to 1,500 yen per person on top of the base rate for the Japan karaoke scene generally, not a figure confirmed directly from Round1's Osaka pricing page, which keeps its exact add-on cost in the same image-graphic format as the base room rate. Worth it if the plan is staying long enough to drink past one order's worth, a bad trade if it's a quick one-song stop that happens to land inside a block that offers it.

## A few policies worth knowing before you go

Round1's page lists a couple of specifics that don't show up on a generic "karaoke in Japan" guide:

- **Guaranteed wait times.** If the floor's busy enough that you're waiting for a room, the store guarantees a block once you're seated, 5 hours on weekdays and the day before a holiday, 4 hours on weekends and holidays, shorter for a solo singer (4 or 3 hours). That's a hard floor on how long a wait-list seating is worth, not an open-ended queue.
- **Kids ride free, sort of.** One room is free per paying adult for elementary and junior high kids (the order minimum still applies), and preschoolers get two free rooms per adult with no order required at all. Worth knowing if a family karaoke stop is on the itinerary rather than a parents-only one like this visit was.
- **Red-price free-time plans don't stack with coupons.** The discounted "red price" blocks the store runs can't be combined with another discount coupon, one or the other, not both.

## What a dedicated karaoke chain charges, for comparison

Big Echo, Japan's biggest standalone karaoke chain, publishes exactly the kind of clean per-30-minute number Round1 doesn't. Rates vary branch by branch, but a few published examples: one Shibuya location lists roughly 190 yen per 30 minutes on a weekday daytime member rate versus 280 yen on weekends, another Shibuya branch runs closer to 225 yen weekdays and 532 yen on weeknights, and a Kabukicho location quotes 155 yen per 30 minutes weekdays with drink-bar access included, jumping to 655 yen on weeknights with an all-you-can-drink deal. None of that is Round1's number, branch pricing at a standalone chain swings just as much as it does at Round1, but it shows the format Round1 is choosing not to use: a plain per-30-minute figure instead of an order minimum plus an unpublished time-block rate.

## Where this fits against general Japan karaoke pricing

Outside any one chain, the general range across Japan's karaoke venues runs roughly 150 to 400 yen per person per 30 minutes on a weekday daytime rate, climbing well past that on weekend evenings depending on the branch, with an all-day weekday free-time plan often landing somewhere around 1,000 to 2,000 yen. Treat that as the shape of what karaoke costs in Japan broadly, not a number pulled from Round1's page, since Round1 keeps its specific rates in image graphics its pricing page doesn't expose as text. If the base room rate matters more than the order minimum for your trip, calling the store directly or checking the banner images on the current page is the way to get the current number for your dates.

## Was it worth it

For a parents' night without the kids, yes, the order minimum alone made this an easy, low-commitment stop rather than something that needed planning around a time block or a drink package. Round1's karaoke floor isn't trying to be a dedicated karaoke bar with a la carte extras, it's one more room in a building that already has bowling and an arcade under the same roof, and the pricing reflects that, less a fixed rate you look up ahead of time, more a minimum you clear once you're in the room. If a full, multi-hour group session with a drink package is the plan, checking the specific store's current banner pricing first is worth the extra step. If it's a quick stop between other plans the way ours was, the one-order minimum is all there is to plan around.
