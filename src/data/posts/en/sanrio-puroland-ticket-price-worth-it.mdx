Cora picked Sanrio Puroland for her sixth birthday, and we built a whole Tuesday in Tokyo around it: a train out to Tama, a day inside, Sushiro for dinner, an arcade stop to close out the night. If you're weighing the same trip, the admission math changed recently, worth knowing before you book.

## What a ticket costs right now

Sanrio Puroland moved to dynamic, date-based pricing, and the top tier went up on August 1 2026. Adult day passes now run 3,900 to 6,900 yen depending on the date, with the new 6,300, 6,600, and 6,900 yen tiers added for the busiest days. High school age and under, plus seniors, run 2,800 to 5,800 yen on the same sliding scale. Kids 2 and under get in free. Those numbers move by date, not a flat rate, so the day of the week and season you pick changes what you pay by a real margin, check the official site or a ticket reseller for your actual date rather than assuming the low end applies.

## The Fast Pass system

Puroland's Fast Pass works differently from a bigger park's: it's sold per ride rather than as one park-wide pass, at machines near each attraction rather than online in advance. Reported prices vary by source, somewhere in the 500 to 2,000 yen range depending on the ride. Whether it's worth buying one at all is a real judgment call: the park only runs two or three major rides, so a popular one can draw a real line, but several guides note that on an ordinary weekday the wait isn't bad outside the single most popular boat ride. Skip it on a quiet weekday, budget for it on a weekend.

## How we spent the day

The train route out of central Tokyo runs from Shinjuku to Tama, with the Sanrio-themed station itself part of the fun before you've even reached the gate. We closed the night at Sushiro, a short trip back toward Shinjuku, an easy pick for picky eaters since conveyor belt sushi lets everyone grab exactly what they want without a group menu negotiation, then an arcade stop at a Shinjuku Gigo location to burn off whatever energy a six year old has left after a full theme park day.

## Was it worth a birthday

Completely, and not because of any one ride. The character meet-and-greets, the photo ops, and a six year old's reaction to the Gudetama sculpture carried the day more than the two or three attractions did. If your kid is deep into a Sanrio character, this is a real day out built for exactly that, not a generic theme park with branding slapped on top.
