April 23, 2026, a night view from our Osaka apartment caught two real landmarks lit up across the water: the Tempozan Ferris Wheel glowing red, and behind it, a cable-stayed bridge lit entirely in pink. We didn't plan around either one, both were just visible out the window after dark.

## The pink wasn't random

The bridge is Tempozan Bridge (天保山大橋), a 640 meter cable-stayed bridge carrying the Hanshin Expressway Bay Route across the Ajikawa River at the mouth of Osaka Port. Its night lighting runs on a real, documented seasonal schedule rather than a fixed color: sakura pink in spring, light blue in summer, gold in autumn, and a warm incandescent tone in winter. Our photo, taken April 23, lands squarely in spring, which is exactly why the bridge reads pink rather than any of its other three seasonal looks. The lighting itself runs on LEDs specifically chosen for low power use, the same system also used on two sister bridges along the same expressway route, Minato Ohashi and Higashi-Kobe Bridge.

## A brief return to a different color scheme

For part of 2025, Tempozan Bridge wasn't on its usual seasonal rotation at all. During the Osaka Expo, which ran through October 2025, the Hanshin Expressway ran a special blue, red, and white lighting display across several Bay Route bridges, Tempozan Bridge included, tied to the Expo's own branding. Our photo was taken the following April, well after the Expo closed, which lines up with the bridge having returned to its normal seasonal palette by then, pink for the real season it was.

## Why there's a port here at all

The river the bridge crosses, the Ajikawa, isn't a natural waterway, it was dug in 1684 as a flood-control project, credited to the engineer Kawamura Zuiken, cutting a new channel to relieve flooding from the Yodo River upstream. Once open, the Ajikawa's mouth grew into one of Osaka's major port gateways, ships from around the country moving goods inland toward Kyoto and Nara along the connected river network. The modern Port of Osaka dates its own founding to July 15, 1868, when the area opened to foreign trade during the Meiji Restoration, old enough that the city marked a 150th anniversary back in 2017. Tempozan Bridge carries a modern expressway across water that's been doing the same basic job, moving ships and goods in and out of Osaka, for well over three centuries.

## What we don't know

We couldn't confirm Tempozan Bridge's original opening year from available sources, only that it carries part of the Bay Route system whose broader construction ran from 1974 through 1994. Rather than guess at a specific year, we're saying so plainly here.

## The Ferris wheel in the same shot

We've already written up the real cost of the Tempozan Ferris Wheel glowing in the same photo, 900 yen a rider, researched rather than field tested on that visit, alongside the free harbor-village sculptures and crab photo ops right at its base. None of that changes here, both landmarks sit in the same stretch of Osaka Port, one you can ride for real yen, the other you can only ever watch change color for free.

## Where to see it for free

Tempozan Bridge and its seasonal lighting are visible from much of the Osaka Port waterfront after dark, no ticket or special vantage point required, ours just happened to be an apartment window a short distance away in Shimaya. Anyone walking the Tempozan Harbor Village waterfront in the evening gets the same free view, bridge and Ferris wheel both lit, before ever deciding whether to pay for a ride or an aquarium ticket.

## Was it worth the view

Completely, and it cost nothing at all. A free night view answered a question we hadn't thought to ask, why a bridge happened to be pink that particular week, and the real answer turned out to be a documented, repeating seasonal system rather than a coincidence, the kind of detail that makes a window view worth looking twice at.
