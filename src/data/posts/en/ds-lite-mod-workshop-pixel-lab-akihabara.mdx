Akihabara has a small cluster of workshops where you build your own retro handheld console from parts rather than buying one already finished, and most of what gets written about them covers the Game Boy version. Pixel Lab Tokyo runs a Nintendo DS Lite specific workshop, and since we haven't done this one ourselves yet, here's the straight, real pricing and what's actually included rather than a personal review.

## What you're actually paying for

- **Build a DS Lite from scratch**, guided by an instructor, rather than buying a pre-modded console. You do the assembly.
- **A new IPS display**, a genuinely visible upgrade over the original DS Lite screen, plus a **USB-C charging port** swapped in for the original proprietary charger, while the workshop keeps the shell and overall feel of the original console.
- **English language support**, and it runs as a small group session rather than a private one-on-one.
- **Listed duration: 2 to 3 hours.**
- The meeting point Klook lists is **Chiyoda Kuritsu Rensei Park**, a municipal park in the Akihabara area.

## The real price

Klook lists this one at **$506.05** for a single adult spot. That's genuinely expensive next to the well known Game Boy and Game Boy Advance mod workshops elsewhere in Akihabara, which typically run somewhere around 50,000 to 53,000 yen (roughly $330 to $350) for a comparable build-it-yourself session. The likely reason: a DS Lite is a fold-open, dual-screen console with a hinge and ribbon cables connecting the two halves, a harder rebuild than a single-screen Game Boy shell, and that shows up in the price.

Worth knowing before you book: this is a brand new listing on Klook, currently sitting at a perfect 5.0 rating but from only 6 reviews, not the thousands of reviews you'd see on an established activity like teamLab. Free cancellation is available up to 48 hours ahead, and booking confirms instantly.
