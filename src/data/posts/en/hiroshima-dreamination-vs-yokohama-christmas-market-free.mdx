Two real winter light events we've visited ourselves, both lit up for roughly the same stretch of the calendar. Hiroshima's Dreamination costs nothing, the whole display, every night of its run. Yokohama's Red Brick Warehouse Christmas Market, which we wrote about separately, looks just as free from a distance, lights visible well past the gate, but the market itself sits behind a paid ticket. If "free winter lights in Japan" is what you're searching for, which one delivers on that depends entirely on which of these two you're picturing.

## Hiroshima's Dreamination: free, start to finish

Dreamination has run every winter since 2002, lighting up Heiwa-odori, Hiroshima's main Peace Boulevard, and the surrounding downtown streets with roughly 1.4 to 1.5 million LED lights across around 500 individual figures and tunnels. Our own visit landed on the festival's 20th year running, with 20 distinct themed sections set up along the route that year specifically, a number tied to the anniversary rather than a fixed yearly count, so don't expect the exact same layout on a different year's visit. No ticket, no gate, no paid zone inside a free one, the entire display is public street lighting you walk through on your own schedule. Hours run 17:30 to 22:30 nightly, and the run typically stretches from mid-November into early January, though exact 2026 dates weren't yet confirmed by the organizers as of this writing, check Hiroshima's own tourism page before you build a night around it.

We went November 28, 2022, during the festival's 20th anniversary year, walking the full stretch of illuminated figures before stopping at the Peace Park to see the eternal peace flame lit up at night, a quieter, more reflective close to the evening than the lights themselves. That flame has real history behind it: lit August 1, 1964, designed by Kenzo Tange, the same architect behind the park's Cenotaph, meant to keep burning until nuclear weapons are abolished worldwide, its base shaped to read as two open palms raised to the sky. It runs on natural gas and burns at roughly double size every August 6, during the 8:15 a.m. moment of silence marking the bombing. Seeing it lit at night, a few streets over from a festival built entirely around colored lights, was its own kind of contrast. Nothing about either stop cost us a yen beyond whatever we ate along the way.

## Getting to each one

Hiroshima is reachable by shinkansen from both Osaka and Tokyo, the kind of long-distance booking decision (JR Pass math, Klook, 12Go, or a station machine) we've already broken down in detail elsewhere on this site. Dreamination itself is centered on Heiwa-odori downtown, walkable from Hiroshima Station or a short tram ride if you're staying further out. Yokohama's market sits at the Red Brick Warehouse on the Shinko waterfront, a short walk from Bashamichi or Sakuragicho Station, already covered in that post's own getting-there section.

## Yokohama's Christmas Market: free zone, paid core

We've already written the real breakdown of this one: a 2026 run from November 20 to December 25, with free illuminated zones surrounding a ticketed main market, general admission 500 to 1,000 yen depending on the date, Priority Entry 2,000 to 2,500 yen. The free side gets you the illuminated YOKOHAMA sign, a view of the market's centerpiece tree, and the general waterfront glow. The German-style huts, the main tree up close, and the food stalls sit behind that gate.

## What this means with kids in tow

Dreamination is an open walk, no gate, no line, no ticket to manage while also managing three kids on a cold night. You move at whatever pace the group wants, stop wherever someone wants a photo, and leave whenever the youngest one is done, no sunk cost tied to a ticket making anyone push through tiredness to "get their money's worth." The Yokohama market's paid core, even at a modest 500 to 1,000 yen, adds the small but real friction of a cash line at the gate during busy evenings, the same crowd-and-coordination challenge we've already written about from our own visit there, worth more planning with a big group than Hiroshima's free walk ever asked of us.

## The real difference

Dreamination is a public lighting display built into the city's own streets, nobody's selling tickets to stand under it. The Yokohama market is a ticketed event that happens to be visually generous to people standing outside the gate, different business models wearing a similar look after dark. If your goal is specifically a zero-cost evening, Dreamination delivers on that completely. If you want market food, huts, and the full scene up close, Yokohama's paid tier gets you that, Hiroshima's festival has no equivalent paid upgrade to compare it against, it's simply a walk.

## Which one fits your trip

Hiroshima makes sense if Dreamination lines up with a trip already headed that way for the Peace Memorial Park and Miyajima, the light display becomes a free evening add-on rather than a reason to detour on its own. Yokohama makes sense if a market atmosphere, food stalls, and a Christmas-market feel specifically is what you're after, and you're fine paying a modest gate fee for the core experience rather than settling for the free periphery. Neither requires the other, they're in different cities on different coasts, but if a winter Japan itinerary happens to pass through both, they're real, different experiences rather than the same seasonal lights twice.

## Was either worth it

Both, for different reasons. Dreamination cost us nothing and gave us a full evening, including a real, moving stop at the peace flame that a paid ticket wouldn't have changed either way. Yokohama's market, paid core and all, gave us the food-stall, huts-and-mulled-wine version of a winter market that Hiroshima's free light walk isn't trying to be. Picking between them isn't really about price, it's about which kind of winter evening you're looking for.
