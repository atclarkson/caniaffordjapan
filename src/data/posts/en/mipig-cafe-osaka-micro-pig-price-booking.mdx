Osaka has a real, operating micro pig cafe, mipig cafe, a reservation-only animal cafe built around time-based sessions with small pigs rather than a drop-in zoo exhibit. It's the venue in our own footage from a 2023 visit, the only dedicated "micro pig cafe" of its kind we could confirm existed in Osaka at the time, and it's still open and bookable today. Here's what a real visit costs and what to know before booking.

## What it costs

mipig's own posted pricing runs on time, not a flat entry fee: a 25-minute session costs 1,870 yen for anyone age 4 and up, a longer 55-minute session costs 2,970 yen. Kids 3 and under get in for 770 yen regardless of which session length the rest of the group books. Want a private room instead of the shared space, that's an extra 550 yen per 25 minutes on top of the base session price. Third-party resellers list different numbers for packaged experiences, Klook's own one-hour listing runs about 3,300 yen and includes a drink and a postcard bundled in, so the gap between booking direct and booking through a reseller isn't really a discount either way, it's a different, bundled product.

## Booking rules worth knowing before you show up

This isn't a walk-in attraction. mipig takes reservations online only, no phone bookings, and sessions do sell out, worth locking in a slot a few days ahead rather than hoping to walk up. The cafe itself houses around 30 pigs rotating through the space, small enough as a group that a fully booked session isn't an overcrowded free-for-all.

## Getting there

The cafe sits in Nishishinsaibashi, Chuo-ku, a short walk from either Shinsaibashi Station (Exit 7) or Yotsubashi Station (Exit 5) on the Osaka Metro, open 10am to 8pm daily. Arrive before your booked time slot starts, the official site is specific that late arrivals eat into the session rather than getting it extended.

## The one restriction most visitors don't expect

A real, easy-to-miss rule: mipig's own site bars entry to anyone unwell, anyone who's entered a hog farm within the past two days, or anyone who's recently been in a country where a hog disease outbreak has been reported, with other listings describing the same restriction in terms of African Swine Fever specifically. Whichever exact disease the rule is written around, the practical effect is the same: it's a biosecurity measure protecting the animals, not an arbitrary rule, and it's exactly the kind of thing that can derail a same-day plan if a pig cafe visit is scheduled for your actual arrival day in Japan. Build in a buffer if this is on your itinerary early in a trip.

## Was it worth it

For a family already building a day around Osaka's animal cafes, yes. The actual draw is simple and doesn't need much selling: a small, calm pig settling into your lap is a different kind of animal encounter than the usual zoo-enclosure distance, and the session-based pricing means you're paying for guaranteed time with the animals rather than a ticket that might get you five minutes if the space is crowded. Book ahead, check the arrival-date restriction if it applies to your trip, and treat the longer 55-minute session as the better value if the group's patience can handle it, the per-minute cost drops noticeably compared to the 25-minute option.
