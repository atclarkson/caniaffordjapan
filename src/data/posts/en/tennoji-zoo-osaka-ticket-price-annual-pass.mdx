Tennoji Zoo has been open since 1915, sits right in the middle of Osaka a few minutes from Tennoji Station, and as of July 2026 costs more to get into than it used to. The adult price jumped from 500 to 800 yen this year, the first real increase in a while, so if you're going off an older blog post or a friend's memory of the price, it's out of date now. We've been three separate times this year alone, so here's what it actually costs, current as of this writing.

## Current admission

| Ticket | Price |
|---|---|
| Adult | 800 yen |
| Elementary or junior high student | 200 yen |
| Preschool age | Free |
| Osaka City resident, 65+ | Free (ID required) |
| Annual pass, adult | 3,200 yen |
| Annual pass, elementary/junior high | 800 yen |

Open 9:30 to 17:00 (last entry 16:00), extended to 18:00 on weekends and holidays in May and September. Closed Mondays.

## What's actually there

About 11 hectares, 170 species, roughly 1,000 animals, genuinely large for a zoo sitting inside a major city rather than out on its edge, and it shows: the giraffe and zebra enclosures back right up against Osaka's actual skyline, so you get modern highrises in the same frame as savanna animals grazing, an odd, good kind of juxtaposition you don't get at most zoos. On our visits we've spent real time at the giraffe enclosure (Cora's favorite animal, and it knows how to work a crowd), watched a male lion do absolutely nothing while two lionesses kept half an eye on visitors, and let the girls pose their faces through the ranger and wolf cutouts on the entrance photo board, a free, low effort stop that's somehow never once failed to get a genuine laugh out of at least one of them.

## Did the annual pass make sense for us

We went in February, again in March, and again in April, three paid adult admissions at 800 yen each, 2,400 yen total for one adult across the year so far. The annual pass costs 3,200 yen, which means it breaks even at exactly four visits (4 times 800 is 3,200). After our third trip we were still 800 yen short of that break-even point, one visit away. If you already know you'll be in Osaka long enough, or visiting often enough, to hit a zoo four times in a year, buy the annual pass up front. If you're not sure, pay per visit and do the math after your third trip the way we just did, the gap to break even is small enough that it's an easy real-time decision rather than a gamble at the ticket counter.

## Getting there

Dobutsuen-mae Station (Osaka Metro Midosuji or Sakaisuji Line) and Ebisucho Station (Sakaisuji Line) are both about a 5 minute walk. JR Tennoji Station and the Midosuji/Tanimachi metro lines put you about 10 minutes out on foot. However you're getting around Osaka that day, the zoo is an easy add-on rather than a special trip out.

## Was it worth it

At 800 yen a visit, this is one of the cheapest full afternoons we've had in Osaka, and it's the one place the girls have asked to go back to three separate times without us suggesting it first. Even with the price increase, it's still a genuinely good value stop, just budget for the new number instead of whatever you may have read before July. If you're staying in Osaka long enough that a fourth visit is even remotely plausible, the annual pass math above makes that decision for you, otherwise pay per visit and don't overthink it.
