Packing for the flight home from Japan is never the same as packing for the flight there. Souvenirs, a second suitcase's worth of snacks, whatever didn't fit in a carry-on on the way in, it all goes into the same bags that already had a weight limit on day one. The airport counter is the worst place to find out you're over it. A handheld digital luggage scale, the kind that clips onto a handle and reads the weight in a couple seconds, costs less than a single overweight fee and tells you the number the night before, while there's still time to do something about it.

## What the fee is

ANA's own current domestic fee page lists the charge per bag, not per trip: 1,100 yen for anything between 23 and 32 kilograms on most routes (500 yen on a few specific routes), and 5,500 yen between 32 and 45 kilograms (4,400 yen on those same exceptions). It's charged at check-in, per piece, with no negotiating once the bag's on the scale.

International routes run higher, several baggage-fee trackers put ANA's international overweight charge around $60 (roughly 6,000 yen) for 23 to 32 kilograms and around $200 (roughly 20,000 yen) for 32 to 45 kilograms, but that didn't load from ANA's own international page to confirm directly, so treat it as a ballpark and check your specific route's current fee before you fly rather than counting on that number. JAL runs its own separate fee schedule; no current rate could be confirmed for this post, so check JAL's own baggage page for your route instead of assuming it matches ANA's.

Either way, the number that matters is the same: a bag that's a kilogram or two over the limit costs money at the counter, money a $10 scale avoids entirely.

## The actual gear

A hook-style digital luggage scale runs about $10 to $15 on Amazon. Hang the strap on the hook, lift the bag a few inches off the floor, and the display reads the weight in a few seconds, no bathroom scale math of weighing yourself, then weighing yourself holding the bag, then subtracting. It's small enough to pack in the bag it's weighing and use again on every future trip.

The timing matters more than the scale itself: weigh at the hotel the night before, not in the taxi line at the airport. Over by a kilogram with a full evening left means moving a sweater into a carry-on. Over by a kilogram at the check-in counter means paying for it.

## The quick version

A bag over the weight limit costs a posted fee at check-in, ANA's own domestic rate alone runs 1,100 to 5,500 yen per bag, more internationally. A $10 to $15 hook scale, used the night before rather than at the airport, is cheaper than one overweight charge and gives you time to fix it instead of just finding out about it at the counter.
