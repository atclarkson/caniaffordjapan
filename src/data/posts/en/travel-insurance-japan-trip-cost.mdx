## A real claim, not a hypothetical

Harper broke her arm in Vienna during our second month of full-time travel, X-rays, a consultation, and a cast, total bill under $300. We paid upfront, submitted the receipts and medical report through SafetyWing's online portal, got confirmation within 24 hours, and had the full reimbursement back within a week. That's a real data point on how the reimbursement side works in practice, not a sales pitch, Vienna isn't Japan and a different injury in a different country could run very differently.

## What the uninsured version costs in Japan specifically

Japan doesn't bill travel insurance directly, you pay the hospital upfront in cash or card and get reimbursed afterward the same way we were in Vienna, so the real risk isn't the insurance paperwork, it's the size of the bill you'd be covering yourself without a policy behind you. One commercial estimate puts an uninsured foreigner's ER visit in Japan at 300,000 to 500,000 yen, roughly $2,000 to $3,300 at typical exchange rates, though that figure comes from a single industry source with no breakdown shown, treat it as a rough ceiling rather than a quoted fact. Some Tokyo hospitals list an upfront emergency deposit in the 100,000 to 150,000 yen range before treatment even starts, refunded against the final bill afterward. One number is solid everywhere: calling an ambulance (119) is free regardless of nationality or insurance status, the bill only starts once you're being treated.

## What this means for a Japan trip specifically

A SafetyWing Essential policy for two adults over a two week Japan trip runs somewhere close to $56 each, call it roughly $112 total with the kids riding free under the group policy's child allowance, against a real Japan ER bill that could land anywhere from a few hundred to several thousand dollars depending on what happens. That gap, a two-week premium smaller than one night out, against an uninsured ER visit that could run into four figures, is the real math worth doing before a trip rather than after one.

## Does Japan require it

No, not for a regular tourist visa or visa-waiver entry, there's no insurance check at the border and no proof required to apply. It's recommended, not mandatory, a different situation from a long-stay or digital nomad visa, where private medical coverage is a real requirement, digital nomad applicants specifically need at least 10 million yen in medical coverage on paper. One more detail worth knowing, single-sourced so treat it as unconfirmed rather than settled fact: reports say Japan has screened arriving travelers since April 2026 for unpaid medical debt from earlier visits, with debts as small as roughly 10,000 yen cited as enough to complicate re-entry. We couldn't independently confirm that against an official government page, but it's one more real reason to settle a Japan medical bill properly, whether through insurance reimbursement or your own cash, rather than letting one go unpaid.
