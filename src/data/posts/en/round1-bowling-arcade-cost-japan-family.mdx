Round1 is the chain you'll spot in almost every Japanese city with a mall attached to it, bowling, an arcade floor, karaoke rooms, sometimes batting cages and a climbing wall, all under one roof. We've been to the Sakai location twice now, once for straight bowling on Lindsay's birthday, once just for the arcade. The pricing works two different ways depending on which one you want.

## Straight bowling

As of a May 2026 nationwide price update, Round1's standard adult bowling rate runs from about 800 yen a game, plus a separate 440 yen shoe rental per person. That's roughly 1,240 yen, about $8, for one game with shoes. Rates shift by store and by day of the week, weekday afternoons tend to run cheaper than weekend evenings, so treat this as a starting point rather than a fixed number.

## Spocha, the all you can play pass

If bowling is one of several things you want to do in one visit, Round1's Spocha pass is usually the better deal. It's a time based ticket that covers the arcade, bowling, and most of the other attractions in the building rather than paying per activity. Per Round1's own pricing page, current visitor rates run:

| Pack | Adult | Student or child |
|---|---|---|
| 90 minutes | from 2,440 yen | from 1,890 yen |
| 3 hours | from 3,090 yen | from 1,890 yen |
| Free time (all day) | from 3,290 yen | from 2,090 yen |

Running over your block costs 100 yen per 10 minutes. Same rule applies here as with straight bowling: these are starting rates, and they vary by store and day.

## Which one to pick

If you know you just want one game of bowling, pay per game, the Spocha packs don't save you anything for a single activity. If you're planning to bounce between bowling, the arcade, and whatever else the location has, the 90 minute Spocha pack usually beats paying for bowling and arcade credit separately once you add it up. For us with three kids at different attention spans, that flexibility has been worth more than the yen difference either way.

Round1 has locations all over Japan, not just Osaka, so this pricing structure is worth knowing wherever your trip takes you, not just in Sakai.
