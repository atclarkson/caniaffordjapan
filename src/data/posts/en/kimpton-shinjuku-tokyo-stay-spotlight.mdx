IHG announced in July 2018 that its boutique Kimpton brand was finally coming to Japan, one property, in Shinjuku. By the time it opened, October 2, 2020, the country's borders were closed to nearly all inbound tourism, deep into pandemic restrictions that wouldn't meaningfully lift for another two years. Japan's first Kimpton hotel opened into a city with almost no tourists left to check into it, the same week Kimpton's Bangkok property opened too, both launching the brand across Asia into the emptiest possible travel market.

This is the first post in a new weekly column, Stay Spotlight, real hotels and unique places to sleep in Japan, researched the same honest way the rest of this site handles prices: real current numbers, sourced from the hotel's own listings, nothing invented. Here's what it offers.

## What's there now

The hotel sits a short walk from Shinjuku Gyoen National Garden, roughly 150 rooms (down from the 162 originally announced in 2018) in an Art Deco-styled boutique building. Michelin's own guide describes the interior as a deliberate blend, classical Japanese design running into a Manhattan boutique-hotel sensibility, less a standard business hotel and more a themed design property. Room sizes in current listings range from around 258 to 753 square feet across King, Twin, and Suite types, with a pillow menu, designer toiletries, bidets, blackout drapes, and down duvets showing up consistently across room descriptions.

## What it costs right now

Recent price snapshots across booking sites show a range of roughly $336 to $509 a night, moving with date and season the way every hotel rate does. Treat that as a ballpark from the moment we checked rather than a quoted price, live availability for your actual dates is the only number worth trusting, and the links below pull real current pricing rather than repeating what we saw today.

## The extras built into the stay

A 24-hour gym and 24-hour room service are standard across listings. Food and drink runs through three named spaces: Jones Café and Bar, the District brasserie, and 86, a rooftop bar, plus four meeting rooms if a stay doubles as a work trip. Breakfast is available but isn't automatically included in every rate, worth checking at booking rather than assuming.

## The rating signal, honestly

Aggregator listings show a 9.6 out of 10 score, a notably high number, but it's built on a small count of reviews split across different room types rather than a large sample, the kind of score that can shift fast with a handful of new reviews either direction. Michelin's own guide lists the property without a score at all, noting it hasn't verified a stay there yet. Both of those are worth knowing before leaning on either number too hard.

## The brand behind the name

Kimpton started in San Francisco in 1981, built around founder Bill Kimpton's own idea that a hotel should feel less anonymous than the category usually allows, boutique design over chain uniformity. IHG bought the company in a deal announced December 2014 and closed in 2015, for $430 million, folding what was then the world's largest independent boutique hotel operator, 62 properties and 71 in-house restaurants and bars, into IHG's own boutique lineup alongside Hotel Indigo and EVEN Hotels. One brand trait worth knowing if it matters to your trip: Kimpton's long-stated policy welcomes pets of any size or species at no extra charge, a real differentiator from most chain hotels, though it's worth confirming current terms for this specific property directly before counting on it.

## Getting there

Shinjuku is one of Tokyo's best-connected stations for a reason, JR lines, the Odakyu and Keio private lines, and four subway lines all meet there, and it's a direct ride from Narita Airport on the JR Narita Express, [about 60 minutes, already written up in full here](/blog/narita-airport-mario-sign-lounge-history-transit-cost/). That connectivity is a real practical point in the hotel's favor beyond the design, a Shinjuku base cuts down on the transfers a less central neighborhood would add to nearly every day trip out of Tokyo.

## Why this one, first

A chain hotel room is a chain hotel room anywhere in the world, the reason to feature a property here is when it's doing something a generic business hotel isn't, a real design identity, a real neighborhood, a real story behind how it got there. A boutique brand's entire country debut landing in the exact week international travel to Japan effectively stopped is the kind of detail worth knowing whether or not you ever book a room here, and Shinjuku Gyoen's garden gates are close enough to make the location a real practical plus if you do.
