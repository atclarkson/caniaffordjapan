## Why we needed them

Tokyo in November runs cooler than a lot of first-time visitors expect, daytime highs commonly sit around 10 to 17 degrees Celsius with evenings dropping to roughly 7 to 12, and late November specifically tends to need real layers, a coat and knitwear rather than just a light jacket. Three kids packed for an earlier, milder stretch of the trip caught up with us exactly the way it usually does, a cold evening and nothing warm enough in the suitcases. The shop itself sits right in the Oshiage neighborhood at the base of Skytree, the same stretch we've already written about for its own free shopping and Kura Sushi flagship, a reminder that a quick retail stop and a tourist landmark can share the exact same block.

## What it likely cost

No receipt survived from this specific stop, so we're not naming an exact total or the store itself. What's real and researchable: affordable Japanese clothing chains commonly carry kids' puffer and down jackets in the 2,000 to 5,000 yen range for a basic insulated style, with name-brand seamless down options running higher, one comparable adult seamless down coat listed around 12,900 yen plus tax at a major Japanese retailer, giving a sense of the ceiling on that style before considering a cheaper kids' cut. Three kids' jackets at the lower end of that range would land somewhere around 6,000 to 15,000 yen total, a real, honest estimate rather than a receipt-backed figure.

## The timing lines up with a real pattern

Japan's big clothing retailers are used to exactly this kind of last-minute purchase. Affordable chains stock basic insulated layers year-round in predictable sizes precisely because a sudden cold snap, or a traveler who simply misjudged the season, is a routine enough occurrence to plan inventory around, not an edge case. A tourist walking in needing three kids' coats in twenty minutes is an easy transaction for a shop built around exactly that kind of quick, practical purchase.

## Was it worth the detour

Completely, and it barely registered as a detour at all, a quick stop on the way to somewhere else rather than a planned shopping trip. We didn't plan for the cold snap, and the real lesson isn't really about coats, it's that checking actual November temperatures before packing would have saved an unplanned stop on a night that was already about to be busy enough.
