Universal CityWalk Osaka is the shopping and dining strip between Universal City Station and the actual USJ gates, and on a rainy April evening this year it turned out to be its own small event before we'd even shown a ticket. Three kids, none of them inclined to walk past anything interesting, turned what could have been a five-minute walk into most of an evening.

## The stops nobody plans for

The first stop wasn't on any list: a restaurant along the strip mounts an enormous tuna sculpture above its entrance, mouth open, and Cora decided on the spot that the correct response was to pose like she was being swallowed by it. We didn't catch the restaurant's name clearly enough to say for certain which one it was, CityWalk has several seafood and sushi spots along that stretch, but the sculpture itself is real, free to photograph, and exactly the kind of oversized restaurant signage Japan does better than almost anywhere else.

A little further down, Harper found the oversized shoe props outside Bubba Gump Shrimp Co., a real American chain restaurant with a genuine location on the fifth floor of CityWalk Osaka. It's a strange thing to encounter a Forrest Gump-themed seafood chain a short walk from a Japanese theme park, but the bench and shoe display are built for exactly the photo Harper took, and it cost nothing to stop and pose. We didn't eat there this time, but the real menu runs from around 1,780 yen for a plate of Cajun shrimp up to 4,520 yen for the largest shared seafood platter, with party plans starting around 4,000 yen a person, prices that track close to what a casual American seafood chain charges back home rather than anything discounted for being overseas.

The last stop was Popcorn Papa, a real flavored-popcorn shop chain marking 25 years in business according to its own storefront sign, all three girls lined up for a photo under it without any prompting from us.

## Getting there doesn't take planning either

Universal City Station sits right at one end of CityWalk, a short ride from Osaka Station on the JR Yumesaki Line (sometimes called the Sakurajima Line), and the walk from the platform to the park gates runs directly through the strip whether you intend to stop or not. There's no separate ticket or entry point for CityWalk itself, it's an open-air shopping and dining complex anyone can walk through, park ticket or not, which is part of why it doesn't register as its own destination the way the park across the plaza does. That's exactly the trap worth avoiding: treating a free, open corridor as nothing more than the path to a paid attraction, when it's stocked with enough real stops to be worth a slower pace on its own.

## Letting the walk be part of the day

None of these three stops were things we researched ahead of time, and that's really the point of how the evening worked. CityWalk sits between the train station and the park entrance, which means every family walking to USJ passes through it whether they plan to stop or not. With three kids under 10, treating that walk as a dead zone to rush through is a losing strategy, someone is going to spot a mascot, a bench, or a sign worth stopping for regardless of the schedule. Building in slack for that rather than fighting it made the fifteen-minute walk into forty-five minutes that felt like a bonus, not a delay.

## Three kids, three different reasons to stop

Part of what made the walk stretch out is that each stop appealed to a different kid for a different reason, not all three ganging up on the same photo op. Cora, the most likely to commit fully to a bit, found the tuna sculpture. Harper, always drawn to anything she can climb on or pose with, claimed the Forrest Gump shoes. The Popcorn Papa stop was the one all three agreed on, a straightforward group photo under a sign with confetti on it. Managing that meant nobody had to compromise on which stop mattered most, everyone got their own moment along the same short stretch of pavement, which is a much easier logistics problem to solve than trying to pick one single highlight for three kids with three different taste in what counts as exciting.

## Was it worth the extra time

Yes, easily. None of these stops cost anything beyond the few minutes it took to pose for a photo, and unlike a lot of what's inside the park gates, there was no ticket, no line, and no decision about whether it fit the day's budget. If USJ is on the itinerary, CityWalk is worth arriving early enough to walk through properly rather than treating it as a corridor, especially with kids young enough that a giant tuna sculpture is its own kind of highlight.
