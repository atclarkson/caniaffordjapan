We went in expecting a quick, cute stop between Harajuku and Shibuya. We left an hour and a half later, all five of us in matching pink smocks and green rubber boots, having spent more time with otters than we'd planned on spending anywhere that day. Harry Harajuku Terrace is not a flat entry fee kind of place, and knowing the pricing structure before you walk in changes how you budget the visit.

## How the pricing actually works

- **Base admission: 1,650 yen per adult, 1,430 yen per child, for 30 minutes.** A drink from the vending machine is included. This covers light interaction, otters wandering the room, the chance to have one climb into your lap if it wants to.
- **"Playing with otters" close contact add-on: about 880 yen**, on top of admission, for closer hands-on time.
- **Treat trays: 500 to 800 yen**, if you want to feed one directly.
- **Children under 3 get in free.**
- As of September 2025, the close contact add-on is restricted to guests 10 and older, younger kids can still be in the room and have an otter approach them, just not the paid hands-on tier.

Thirty minutes moves fast once you're actually in the room, otters are doing something new every couple of minutes and nobody's watching the clock. That's the real budgeting trap here: it's easy to think you're paying for "a stop at the otter cafe" when you're actually paying per half hour, and it's worth deciding ahead of time whether you're doing one block or planning to stay for two.

## What a family visit actually looks like

Everyone gets a smock, an apron, and rubber boots at the door, not optional, and it's part of why the room stays as clean as it does with several otters loose in it at once. The girls were locked in the entire time, feeding, petting, one otter falling asleep directly in Harper's lap at one point. It's a genuinely hands-on setup, not a look-but-don't-touch enclosure, which is exactly why the time limit matters more than the entry price does.

## Was it worth it

For us, yes, without hesitation, it's one of the girls' favorite things we did in Tokyo. Budget for more than one 30 minute block if you have young kids who won't want to leave on a timer (ours didn't), skip the close contact add-on if anyone in your group is under 10 since it's restricted now anyway, and go in knowing the real cost is closer to 2,000 to 2,500 yen per person once you add a treat tray or a second block, not the base 1,650.
