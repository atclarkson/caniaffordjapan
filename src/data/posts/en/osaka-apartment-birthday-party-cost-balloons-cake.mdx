We've already written about Cora's 9th birthday at Universal Studios Japan, the park day, the ride logistics, the three-kids-three-agendas problem. That post mentioned in passing that the actual birthday happened the day before, in the family's Osaka apartment, gifts and an ice cream cake and a safari-themed balloon setup, no park admission involved at all. Here's what that quieter half of the celebration cost.

## The cake: 3,300 to 4,000 yen

We don't have a receipt for this specific cake, so we're not claiming an exact number, but it matches a real, common category in Japan: an ice cream cake from 31 (Baskin-Robbins' name here, written "Thirty One" or サーティワン), whose character and holiday-themed ice cream cakes currently run 3,300 to 4,000 yen depending on the design. It's a real, easy option for a birthday that doesn't require a bakery order placed days in advance, pick one up from a nearby store the same day and it's ready to serve.

## The balloons and banner: roughly 2,500 to 2,800 yen

The safari garland, the giraffe and lion foil balloons, the jungle-animal banner, all sourced online rather than from a specialty party store. Themed balloon and banner sets in that style currently list for roughly 2,500 to 2,800 yen on Japanese marketplaces, cheap enough that decorating an entire apartment corner costs less than the cake sitting in front of it.

## The wrapping: whatever kraft paper costs

Every gift on the table came wrapped in plain brown kraft paper rather than printed birthday wrapping paper, a cheap, widely available option at Japanese stationery and 100 yen shops that also happens to look deliberately styled next to a brightly colored balloon arrangement, no extra cost for the aesthetic, just the cheapest paper on the shelf doing double duty.

## Why split the cake day from the park day

We already covered the logistics reasoning in the USJ post: splitting the actual birthday from the theme park day meant nobody was overtired from a party the same morning they needed energy for a full park day, and neither half had to compete with the other for attention. The cost version of that same logic is simpler. A home party this cheap doesn't need to be bundled with an expensive park day to feel like a real celebration, and keeping them separate meant the ice cream cake and balloon corner didn't get lost as a footnote to the bigger, pricier day that followed.

## Was it worth it

Completely, and for under 7,000 yen combined for the cake, the decorations, and the wrapping, cheaper than a single adult USJ ticket the next day. A home birthday doesn't need a theme park budget behind it to be the better-photographed half of the week, and this one, jungle banner, kraft paper, ice cream cake, kept more of the attention than the globe at the park entrance.
