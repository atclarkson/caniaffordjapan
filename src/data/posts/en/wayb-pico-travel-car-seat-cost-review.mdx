## What it is

The Pico is a folding, fabric-shell travel car seat built to be light enough to carry rather than check, the alternative to lugging a full-size convertible seat through an airport or renting one at a counter that may or may not have your size in stock. It folds flat into its own bag for the overhead bin or a checked backpack.

## FAA approved, which matters more than it sounds like

The Pico carries FAA approval for use as an airplane seat, not just a car seat, the sticker confirming it sits on the bottom of the shell. That dual use is the real reason it earns a spot in the carry-on rotation instead of the checked bag: the same seat that keeps a toddler secured in a rental car also straps into an airplane seat, one piece of gear covering both legs of a trip instead of two.

## Where this matters most for a Japan trip

We've [written up Japan's real car seat law separately](/blog/japan-child-car-seat-law-taxi-rental-car/): licensed taxis are exempt, which covers most of what a Japan trip built around trains and city taxis needs. A rental car is where the Pico earns its keep specifically, Japan's under-6 requirement applies to rental cars the same as a private vehicle, and rental counters charge a real per-day or per-rental fee for a seat they supply, assuming they have your size in stock when you land. Bringing a folding seat that's already sized right and already familiar to a kid who's napped in it on a dozen flights removes that uncertainty for a Hakone or countryside leg of a trip.

## Owning one versus renting locally every time

The honest comparison isn't the Pico against nothing, it's the Pico against just paying the rental counter's fee each time a trip needs a car. Toyota Rent a Car's own pricing, which we've [confirmed separately](/blog/japan-child-car-seat-law-taxi-rental-car/), charges a one-time 1,650 yen option fee for a child or infant seat, call it roughly $11 at typical exchange rates. One Japan trip with one rental car leg, that's obviously the cheaper call by a wide margin. The math flips once a family travels often enough that "one trip" isn't the real unit: four years in, we've used the same seat across enough flights and rental cars in enough countries that paying a local fee every single time would have added up past the Pico's own price a long time ago, on top of the real risk of a counter not having the right size in stock on a given day.

## Four years in, was it worth it

Yes, and the real evidence is that it's still in rotation rather than replaced. A seat bought for one toddler on one departure day has carried two more kids through the same weight range since, across flights, rental cars, and however many countries a family of five ends up adding up over four years of full-time travel. The $499 starting price reads steep next to a $30 inflatable booster, but measured against years of actual use instead of one trip, it's the rare piece of travel gear that's earned the space it takes up in a bag.
