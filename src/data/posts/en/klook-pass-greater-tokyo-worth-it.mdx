The Klook Pass Greater Tokyo lets you pick 2 to 7 attractions from a list that includes Tokyo Skytree, Tokyo Tower, teamLab Borderless or Planets, Sanrio Puroland, and a handful of others, pay one bundled price, then redeem each one within a 90 day window after your first visit. The pitch is always "save up to X%," but whether that's actually true depends entirely on which attractions you pick, so we ran the math on our own with current prices instead of taking Klook's percentage at face value.

## The pass pricing tiers

| Attractions | Adult |
|---|---|
| 2 | $46.25 |
| 3 | $68.85 |
| 4 | $81.75 |
| 5 | $93.55 |
| 6 | $109.69 |
| 7 | $133.35 |

These are the most recently documented tiers we could confirm and may have shifted since, Klook's pass pricing moves over time same as everything else on their platform, so treat this table as a reference point and check the live price before buying, not a locked-in number.

## What three of the "standard" attractions actually cost separately

We've been to both Tokyo Skytree and Tokyo Tower, and covered teamLab Planets in its own post already, so we have real current prices for all three without guessing:

| Attraction | Price alone |
|---|---|
| Tokyo Skytree, Tembo Deck (350m) | 1,800 yen weekday / 2,100 yen weekend (about $11.50 to $13.50) |
| Tokyo Tower, Main Deck | about 1,500 yen (about $9.60) |
| teamLab Planets | $24.29 |
| **Total, booked separately** | **about $45 to $47** |

Against the pass's 3-attraction tier at $68.85, booking these three individually comes out cheaper, by a real margin, not a rounding error. Skytree's ticketing system moved to dynamic pricing in April 2026 and came in lower than it used to be, and teamLab Planets has never been expensive to begin with, so a combo built entirely from the pass's cheaper standard-tier options doesn't clear the bar.

## Where the pass actually wins

The math flips once a premium add-on enters the mix. The pass's premium tier includes things like a single Tokyo Disneyland or DisneySea park ticket, Warner Bros. Studio Tour Tokyo, or a Mt. Fuji day excursion, all of which run well past $50 to $70 on their own. Pair one of those with a couple of the cheaper standard attractions and the bundle math works in your favor, because the expensive item is what's actually being discounted, the cheap ones are just along for the ride.

The pass is a genuine deal when it includes something expensive. It's a break-even or a loss when it's built entirely out of attractions that were already affordable on their own.

Any version of this pass clears the $50 cart minimum on its own once you're at the 3-attraction tier or above, so the general new and existing user discount tiers apply directly, on top of whatever the pass itself already saves you.

## The actual takeaway

Before buying, price out your specific attraction list individually first. If the total comfortably beats the matching pass tier, book direct and skip the pass. If one premium attraction is dragging the individual total up past what the pass costs, the pass wins and the convenience of one checkout is a genuine bonus on top.
