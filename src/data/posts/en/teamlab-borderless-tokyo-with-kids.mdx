teamLab Borderless closed its original Odaiba location in 2022 and reopened in Azabudai Hills in February 2024. We went that same summer with all three girls, Lily, Cora, and Harper, and it's still one of the only attractions in Tokyo where none of our kids asked to leave early.

Borderless is a set of dark rooms full of projected, moving light art that responds to where you stand and how you move through it. There's no fixed path. You wander, and the art follows you as much as you follow it.

## What actually worked for us with kids

- **No set route.** With three different attention spans, not being locked into a single walking path meant everyone could linger where they wanted and skip what bored them.
- **The orb room is the moment.** Hundreds of suspended, glowing light orbs that shift color as you move through them. This is the room every photo of Borderless is actually from, ours included.
- **It's dark and disorienting in places.** If you've got a kid who doesn't love the dark, know that going in. Ours were fine, but it's worth mentioning before you buy tickets.
- **Go on a weekday if you can.** It's popular enough that weekend crowds change the experience, less wandering, more shuffling.
