## Why a stroller this small exists

Most strollers fold down to something you still have to lean against a wall or wedge into a car trunk. The Pockit line exists to solve a narrower problem: fitting in an overhead bin, a train's luggage rack, or a coin locker, the exact spaces a Japan trip puts a family through constantly. It trades size for comfort in a pretty direct way, and whether that trade is worth it depends entirely on how much of the trip involves trains, elevators, and narrow sidewalks versus open stroller-friendly parks.

Sources disagree slightly on the exact weight and folded dimensions, a few tenths of a pound and an inch or so either way depending on which retailer's listing you check, treat the numbers above as a close real range rather than one locked figure. One more honest note: our own real product link points to the original Pockit All-City, while most current reviews and retail listings cover the newer Pockit+ All-City, GB's updated version. The two are close enough in size and weight that the folding math above holds for either, but if a spec matters down to the exact inch for your specific trip, confirm which version a listing is selling before buying.

## The company behind the name

GB is a brand under Goodbaby International, a Hong Kong-listed company founded in 1989 that also owns the premium German brand Cybex (acquired in 2014) and the US brand Evenflo. Goodbaby became China's top-selling stroller maker by 1993 and the largest stroller supplier in the US market by 1999, before the 2014 acquisitions shifted the group from a manufacturing-first company into a brand-driven one. GB itself is now the smaller, domestic-facing brand in that portfolio next to Cybex's bigger international push, known specifically for the ultra-compact Pockit line rather than full-size strollers. That history doesn't change what the Pockit does, but it's worth knowing this isn't a no-name product, it's a long-running stroller manufacturer's answer to one specific problem: folding small.

## What an independent review found

BabyGearLab, a pediatrician-led gear review site, rated it 73 out of 100, ranked 4th out of 12 travel strollers they tested, with the compact fold (8.4 out of 10) as the clear strength and kid comfort (6.2) and maneuverability (5.5) as the weak points. Their stated bottom line: "a super small folded stroller that is otherwise largely average." The specific complaints worth knowing before buying: a frame that flexes more than sturdier (and bulkier) strollers, push handles reviewers called awkward, and no peek-a-boo window in the sunshade, a small thing until a kid falls asleep and you want to check on them without stopping.

## Where it earns its keep on a Japan trip

The real test isn't a backyard, it's a train platform with a departure board counting down, or a zoo exit corridor like the one in the photo above. A stroller that folds flat enough to carry through a ticket gate or wedge into a coin locker removes a genuine daily friction point that a bulkier jogging-style stroller creates on a trip built around trains rather than a car. It's also realistic about tradeoffs: a flexier frame and less plush ride matter less on smooth train-station tile and sidewalk than they would on rougher terrain, which is exactly the kind of ground a Japan city trip mostly covers.

## Does it fit a coin locker

A real, Japan-specific test of "folds small": JR East's own sample large-locker dimensions run about 84 cm high, 34 to 43 cm wide, and 57 cm deep (sizes vary station to station, treat this as a representative range rather than a guarantee at every locker bank). The Pockit's roughly 21 x 13 x 8 inch fold works out to about 53 x 33 x 20 cm, comfortably under the height limit and close to the narrow end of the width range, tight in a standard large locker but realistically fits where a full-size stroller simply wouldn't. That's the real use case this size category is built around, not just an overhead bin on a flight but the exact lockers a Japan day trip runs through constantly.

## How it compares to the other "folds small" pick on this site

We've [written up the Wayb Pico car seat](/blog/wayb-pico-travel-car-seat-cost-review/) for the same reason we're writing this one up: both are real pieces of our own travel kit built around the same core idea, fold small enough to carry rather than check or rent locally. The two solve different problems though, the Wayb handles car seat requirements for taxis and rental cars, this handles getting a toddler through a train station without a bulky stroller slowing the whole group down. A family doing both trains and the occasional rental car leg of a Japan trip has a real case for both, not either-or.

## Who should skip it

A family mostly renting a car, staying in one place, or covering ground a full-size stroller handles better (parks, beaches, uneven ground) gets less value out of paying a premium for a fold this compact. The Pockit is solving a transit-and-space problem specifically, not building a more comfortable ride than a standard stroller at a lower price, and BabyGearLab's own numbers back that up: it wins on size, not on kid comfort or handling.

## The honest verdict

Worth it for what it's built for: a trip where the stroller spends real time being folded, carried, and stashed rather than just pushed. A Japan trip, trains, platforms, elevators, coin lockers, is close to the ideal use case this product was designed around, which is the real reason it's shown up in our own travel footage more than once rather than sitting unused in a closet.
