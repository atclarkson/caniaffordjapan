A different real day of ours in Ueno, tracked to the dollar on screen rather than estimated afterward: $315.49 total, zoo included, swan boats included, an Ameyoko market stop included. We've [already written up a confirmed Day 1 total from a separate $300/day week](/blog/tokyo-300-dollar-day-budget-week-reality-check/), $401.34, and this is not that same trip, it's a different real visit with its own itemized numbers, worth laying out on its own rather than folding into that post and implying they're the same week.

## What was free

Walking Ameyoko itself costs nothing, no gate, no ticket, just a crowded strip of stalls between Ueno and Okachimachi stations that we've [already written up for its food prices and disputed name origin](/blog/ameyoko-name-origin-ueno-food-stalls-cost/). Ueno Park's paths, the historic Former Entrance and main gates, and simply walking the grounds around the zoo and Shinobazu Pond are also free, the same free-to-walk pattern that shows up across most of Tokyo's park land. None of that shows up as a line item on the day's balance sheet because none of it cost anything to do.

## What cost money, itemized

The video's own on-screen "Day 3 Balance Sheet" breaks the paid side down this way:

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Accommodation | ¥27,475 ($175.84) |
| Dinner | ¥8,510 ($54.46) |
| Lunch | ¥5,640 ($36.10) |
| Snacks/grocery | ¥2,740 ($17.54) |
| Swan boats | ¥1,600 ($10.24) |
| Transportation | ¥1,680 ($10.75) |
| Ueno Zoo | ¥1,200 ($7.68) |
| Miscellaneous | ¥450 ($2.88) |
| **Total** | **¥49,295 ($315.49)** |

We've [already published the real per-ticket prices for the zoo and the boats separately](/blog/halloween-costumes-cost-tokyo-ueno-zoo-shinobazu/), 600 yen for an adult zoo ticket and 700 yen for a 30-minute swan boat. The totals here run a bit higher than either single price alone, which tracks: a family total covering multiple tickets and more than one boat naturally lands above a single adult admission, not a contradiction of the number already on the site, just a different unit, a day's family spend rather than one person's ticket.

## The line that drives the number

Accommodation is more than half this day's total on its own, $175.84 of $315.49. That's the same pattern [the Day 1 post already flagged](/blog/tokyo-300-dollar-day-budget-week-reality-check/): a nightly lodging cost gets charged against whichever day it happens to land on in a running counter, even though it's not really that day's spending, it's the trip's fixed cost showing up wherever the math gets tallied. Strip accommodation out and this day's real activity-and-food spend was $139.65, zoo, boats, two meals, snacks, and transit for a family of five, a far more useful number than the headline total if what you're trying to budget is a day out rather than a hotel bill.

## Two data quirks worth flagging rather than hiding

Not every number from this family's own video descriptions held up on close reading. Two other videos in the same loosely day-numbered Tokyo series, one covering Odaiba, one covering Asakusa, both list an identical "Day 1 Balance Sheet," same yen figures down to the cent, even though the Asakusa video's own title calls itself Day 2. That reads like a copy-paste error carried from one video description to the next rather than two different real days that happened to cost exactly the same, so neither of those numbers appears anywhere on this site. Separately, the ¥27,475 accommodation figure on this Ueno day's own sheet is the exact same number already cited in [the Day Zero cost breakdown post](/blog/landing-in-tokyo-day-zero-cost-breakdown/), sourced from a different video entirely, filmed on a different real trip months apart. That could mean a similar nightly rate across two separate Tokyo stays, or it could mean this family's video-description template reuses a stock accommodation figure rather than recalculating it for every clip. We can't tell which from the data alone, so we're naming it rather than treating the lodging line as independently confirmed. The rest of this day's numbers, zoo, boats, meals, transit, snacks, don't repeat anywhere else across either family of posts, which is the real reason we're building this post around them.

## Was it worth using

Yes, with the caveats stated plainly above rather than smoothed over. A real, itemized day of family spending in Tokyo, zoo, boats, market, two meals, is useful budgeting data on its own, and $139.65 for everything except the room is a reassuring number for anyone picturing Ueno as an expensive stop. Treat the lodging line the same way we already recommended treating arrival-day logistics: as a fixed cost to budget for the trip as a whole, not something that should make any single day's activities look pricier than they really were.
