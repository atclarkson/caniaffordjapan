A standard suitcase, Yamato Transport's Size 160 category (up to 160cm in combined dimensions, up to 25kg), costs 2,409 yen to ship within the same prefecture, and every parcel sent to an airport adds a flat 660 yen on top of that. That's the number most guides blur into a vague "2,000 to 3,200 yen" range. Here's the size-based table behind it.

## The rate table, by size

Yamato prices by whichever is bigger, total linear dimension or weight, not by suitcase type. These are the company's same-prefecture rates, tax included, paid cashless:

| Size category | Up to | Up to | Price (cashless) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 60 | 60cm | 2kg | 781 yen |
| 80 | 80cm | 5kg | 1,089 yen |
| 100 | 100cm | 10kg | 1,408 yen |
| 120 | 120cm | 15kg | 1,727 yen |
| 140 | 140cm | 20kg | 2,090 yen |
| 160 | 160cm | 25kg | 2,409 yen |
| 180 | 180cm | 30kg | 3,025 yen |
| 200 | 200cm | 30kg | 3,685 yen |

A typical large checked suitcase (around 70-75cm tall, close to a standard airline's size limit) usually lands in the Size 160 or Size 180 bracket. Every rate in this table already includes tax; paying cash instead of cashless adds a few yen per tier.

## The part every guide skips: the airport fee

Yamato's page for sending parcels to an airport states plainly that its listed rates "include fees incurred when sending parcels to the airport," a flat 660 yen baked into the total. So a Size 160 bag shipped to an airport in the same prefecture as your hotel runs 2,409 yen plus that 660 yen, close to 3,069 yen all in, which lines up with third-party price-check sites quoting around 3,070 yen for the same size class. Haneda sits inside Tokyo itself, so a Tokyo hotel shipping there qualifies for the same-prefecture rate. Narita is in Chiba Prefecture, a different prefecture from most Tokyo hotels, so that route uses Yamato's inter-prefecture rate instead, a different number this table doesn't cover. Check Yamato's rate calculator for your specific hotel-to-airport route before counting on the same-prefecture figure.

## Shaving a few hundred yen off

Yamato stacks a handful of small discounts on top of the base rate: 100 yen off for dropping the bag at a convenience store, a TA-Q-BIN Agency, or a sales office instead of a home pickup; another 60 yen off for paying through its digital options; and 200 yen off if you book a round trip, sending a bag out and getting a matching one back. Sign up as a Kuroneko Member and the discount runs 10 to 15% off the whole shipment. None of these stack to a dramatic total, but on a 2,409 yen shipment, the convenience store discount alone is close to a 4% cut for doing nothing but carrying the bag one extra block.

Every parcel is covered up to 300,000 yen if it's lost or damaged, and the hard ceiling is 30kg, heavier than that and Yamato won't take it at any price.

## Where you can send from, and when

Yamato runs send counters at every major Japanese airport: both Haneda and Narita terminals, Kansai, Chubu Centrair, Fukuoka, New Chitose, and a dozen more regional airports. You can also start the shipment from a hotel, a Yamato sales office, a TA-Q-BIN Agency, or a convenience store, each of the last three knocks 100 yen off the price. Timing runs on a deadline, not a same-day guarantee: Yamato's page says to drop a bag off by two or three days before departure for standard delivery to the airport, not the morning of. Yamato runs a dedicated same-day service too, but it's a separate product from standard TA-Q-BIN and isn't priced on the same table.

Yamato's English-language phone line runs 8:00am to 9:00pm, 365 days a year (03-6834-7503), worth having saved if a drop-off deadline or a counter's hours don't match what's posted online. For the other direction, airport to hotel, [our day-zero Tokyo cost breakdown](/blog/landing-in-tokyo-day-zero-cost-breakdown/) has a number from an actual family receipt: 6,926 yen for a family of five's worth of bags on arrival.

## Was it worth including

For a family checking out of a hotel with more suitcases than hands, not hauling full-size luggage through a train transfer to the airport is worth the 2,000 to 4,000 yen range this table covers. The number to plan around is the deadline, not the price, since this isn't a same-day service in the direction you're leaving Japan.
