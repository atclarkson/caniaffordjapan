Walking Dotonbori at night doesn't cost anything, and it's still one of the best things we've done anywhere in Japan. The canal, the wall of neon signs, the giant mechanical crab and blowfish hanging over the street, and the famous Glico Man billboard: none of it has a ticket, a gate, or a reason to rush past it.

We went with all three girls, Lily, Cora, and Harper, and they were just as locked in as we were. Dotonbori at 8pm on a spring night is loud, bright, and completely free to just walk through.

## What's actually free here

- **The Dotonbori canal boardwalk**, open all night, no entry, no closing time.
- **The Glico Man billboard**, running since 1935 and still the single most photographed spot in Osaka. Standing under it costs nothing.
- **The giant animal signs**, the crab, the blowfish, the octopus, hanging over storefronts up and down the street. Half the fun of Dotonbori is just looking up.
- **Sōemon-chō**, the quieter canal-side strip one block over, its own string of lanterns and neon reflected in the water, and noticeably fewer crowds than the main drag.

## Where the actual money goes: dinner

Free canal, but the family still needs to eat, and this is where Dotonbori and Sōemon-chō start costing something. We ended up canal-side in Sōemon-chō for dinner and drinks. We didn't keep the receipt from this particular stop, so instead of inventing a number, here's what's generally true of eating in this district rather than what our exact bill was: a draft beer at a casual spot typically runs somewhere around 500 to 700 yen, and shared plates (yakitori, karaage, that kind of thing) are usually 800 to 1,500 yen each. For a table of five with a couple of drinks and enough food to go around, that's a realistic range to plan for, not a fixed number.

## What an evening in Dotonbori actually costs

| Thing | Cost |
| --- | --- |
| Walking Dotonbori and Sōemon-chō, canal to canal | $0 |
| Photos with the Glico Man sign and the giant crab | $0 |
| A beer at a casual riverside spot | ~$4 to $5 |
| A shared plate (yakitori, karaage) | ~$5 to $10 |

The walk itself is the whole reason to come. Dinner is optional, and the price of it is entirely up to how many rounds you order, not the neighborhood charging you to be in it.
