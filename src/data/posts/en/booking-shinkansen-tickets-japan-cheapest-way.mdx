Four ways to book a shinkansen ticket in Japan: the JR Pass, a station ticket machine, Klook, or 12Go. Which one saves you money depends entirely on how much ground you're covering, not on which option sounds the most official.

## The JR Pass, by the numbers

JR raised the nationwide pass price on October 1, 2026. A 7-day Ordinary pass is now 53,000 yen, up from 50,000. The 14-day runs 84,000 yen, the 21-day 105,000 yen, and Green Car (first class) adds roughly 20,000 yen to each tier. Kids 6 to 11 pay half, under 6 ride free on your lap.

That pass covers unlimited rides on JR lines nationwide, including most shinkansen, but not the fastest Nozomi or Mizuho services. Riding Nozomi with a JR Pass means paying a roughly 4,960 yen supplement on top, or taking the slightly slower Hikari or Kodama instead, both fully covered, both maybe 10 to 40 minutes longer depending on the route.

## When the pass pays for itself

A single Tokyo to Osaka round trip on a reserved Nozomi seat runs about 29,000 yen total. That alone doesn't come close to earning back a 53,000 yen pass. The math only works once you're covering real distance across a trip: Tokyo to Kyoto, then on to Hiroshima, then back to Tokyo, for example, where three separate long-distance legs stack up fast against a flat pass price. A single city-pair round trip almost never justifies the pass. A multi-city loop usually does.

For a family of five, run the math per person rather than assuming the pass is automatically the family-friendly choice. Two adults and three kids aged 6 to 11 on that same Tokyo-Kyoto-Hiroshima-Tokyo loop: each adult pass is 53,000 yen, each kid's pass is half that at 26,500 yen, for a household total of 185,500 yen. Point-to-point reserved tickets for the same three legs, at roughly 13,000 to 15,000 yen per adult per leg and half that per child, land somewhere in the same range once you add it up, sometimes a little under, sometimes a little over depending on exactly which stations and trains you book. The pass buys flexibility, not a guaranteed discount, so price out your actual stations before assuming it's the cheaper call for a family specifically.

## Booking without a pass

Three real ways to buy a point-to-point ticket:

- **JR's own machines or SmartEX**, at any station, no advance booking required for an unreserved seat, cash or card. The baseline every other option gets compared against.
- **Klook** sells shinkansen e-tickets directly, listing fares from $84.29 for Tokyo to Kyoto and $87.79 for Tokyo to Osaka, both of which convert to less than JR's own quoted reserved-seat fare for the same routes. Most routes deliver a QR code you scan straight at the gate, a few require picking up a physical ticket, and Klook's site flags which is which before you pay. Klook also sells the JR Pass itself, listed from $221 to $321 depending on the pass, worth comparing against the official site's yen price at the exchange rate on the day you book.

## Seats and luggage

A reserved seat costs 330 to 930 yen more than unreserved, per Klook's own published fare breakdown, and guarantees a specific seat rather than first-come standing room in the unreserved cars during peak travel. For five people traveling together, booking reserved seats ahead through Klook, 12Go, or SmartEX means getting seated as a group rather than splitting up across a packed unreserved car. One more real detail worth knowing before you pack: oversized luggage on the Tokaido, Sanyo, and Kyushu shinkansen lines needs its own reservation, a flat 1,000 yen fee applies if you board with a large bag unreserved, and bags over 250cm aren't allowed on board at all.

## How far ahead you can book

JR's own station machines and SmartEX sell tickets same day, no advance window needed, you just can't guarantee a seat on a packed train that way. Klook allows booking up to 90 days ahead, the longest window of the four, useful if you're locking in an itinerary months before the trip. 12Go has historically required a shorter advance window, closer to a few days out rather than months, worth checking their current checkout page before counting on same-day availability through that channel specifically. None of the four require booking the instant tickets go on sale, but during cherry blossom season, Golden Week, or any other major holiday stretch, booking weeks rather than days ahead through Klook or 12Go is the difference between getting the train you want and standing in the unreserved car.

## Which one for your trip

One or two long-distance legs: book Klook or the station machine directly, skip the pass. A multi-city loop hitting three or more long-distance legs: run the JR Pass math first, it usually wins. Traveling with a group where everyone needs seats together during a busy season: book ahead through Klook or 12Go rather than counting on same-day availability at the machine. Flying solo with a flexible schedule and no reserved-seat requirement: the unreserved line at the station costs nothing extra to use and needs no advance planning at all.

## What we use day to day

Our own Japan travel has stayed local, Osaka's subway, Tokyo's metro, the Hakone Tozan Line in the photo above, all paid for with an IC card rather than a shinkansen ticket.
