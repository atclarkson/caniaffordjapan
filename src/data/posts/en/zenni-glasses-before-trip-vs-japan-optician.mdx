Here's both, broken down line by line, so you can see which one fits your actual situation.

## Option 1: Zenni, ordered before you fly

- **Frame**: $6.95 to $49.95, depending on style. The cheapest frames are real, not a bait price, but the selection narrows fast once you filter for anything beyond the most basic shape.
- **Lenses**: a basic single-vision lens with a 1.50 index, anti-scratch coating, and UV protection is bundled into that starting price. Anything beyond that, a thinner high-index lens, blue light filtering, photochromic tint, adds on separately and moves the total up quickly.
- **Shipping**: standard runs around $4.95 with delivery in 14 to 21 days, priority around $8.45 in 10 to 16 days, express around $18.95 in 8 to 12 days. These are Zenni's own published tiers, not guaranteed, and worth confirming on their site before you order since shipping pricing is the kind of thing that changes without much notice.
- **What you need before you start**: a current prescription, either a PD (pupillary distance) measurement from your own optometrist or self-measured using Zenni's own guide. No prescription on hand, no order.
- **Total for a basic pair**: realistically $20 to $60 once you add a reasonable lens upgrade to the base frame price, before shipping. Zenni doesn't take vision insurance, but does accept FSA and HSA cards as payment.
- **Getting your PD measurement**: if your last eye exam's prescription doesn't list a pupillary distance, which most US exams skip unless you ask for it, Zenni's site walks you through measuring it yourself with a ruler and a mirror, or a credit card held against your face for scale. It's not hard, but it's one more step to do before you're ready to order, not something you can skip.
- **Returns**: a 30-day window, with a 100% in-store credit refund or a 50% cash refund. Worth knowing before you order a pair sight-unseen, since the return math isn't a full refund if the fit or prescription turns out wrong.

## Option 2: A walk-in optician once you land

- **Exam**: free, bundled into the glasses, no appointment needed, roughly 15 to 20 minutes at any JINS, Zoff, or OWNDAYS location. No existing prescription required to walk in.
- **Glasses**: JINS runs 5,500 to 13,200 yen, Zoff has three flat tiers at 5,500, 8,800, and 13,200 yen, OWNDAYS runs 9,800 to 25,300 yen. Frame and standard lenses included in every one of those numbers.
- **Turnaround**: about 30 minutes for in-stock lenses, meaning exam and finished glasses can happen in the same afternoon.
- **What you need before you start**: nothing. No prescription, no measurement, no appointment.
- **Total for a basic pair**: roughly 5,500 to 13,200 yen at the cheaper chains, somewhere around 35 to 85 dollars at typical exchange rates, done same day.

## What ordering from Zenni involves

1. Upload a photo of your prescription, or enter the numbers by hand if you don't have a copy to photograph.
2. Enter your PD, measured yourself or pulled from the prescription if it's listed.
3. Pick a frame, then pick lens options: index, coating, tint, blue light filtering if you want any of it.
4. Pay with a card, FSA, or HSA, and pick a shipping tier.
5. Wait the quoted window, 8 to 21 days depending on which tier you chose, track the order, and plan for the pair to land before you leave rather than during the trip.

None of that happens in person, which is exactly the appeal for anyone who already knows their prescription and would rather not spend a travel afternoon at an optician. It's also exactly where it falls apart for anyone who doesn't have a current prescription in hand, since steps 1 and 2 can't happen without one.

## The difference that matters

Zenni's lower bound looks cheaper on paper, but it assumes you already have a current prescription in hand and that two to three weeks of lead time works with your schedule. Neither is guaranteed on a trip. A kid who outgrows a prescription, breaks a pair, or needs glasses for the first time mid-trip has no two-week window to wait on a shipment, that's exactly the gap the walk-in chains fill, and it's why our own post on Lily's exam came from an unplanned need, not something ordered ahead of time.

There's a quality tradeoff underneath the price too, one we can't verify firsthand since we haven't ordered from Zenni ourselves. Independent reviews describe the frames as fine for the price but inconsistent on fit, and a step down from what an optometrist-fitted pair feels like, with some buyers reporting the wrong prescription arriving and having to go through the return process above to fix it. The walk-in Japan chains skip that risk entirely, the exam and the fitting happen in person, in the same visit, with staff adjusting the frame on your face before you leave the store.

Where Zenni wins outright: you know your prescription, you know you need new glasses, and you're not in a hurry. Order before you fly, it arrives at home with time to spare, and you walk into the trip with a spare pair already packed. That's a real use case, just a different one from what sent us to an Osaka optician in the first place.

## What we'd do differently next time

Knowing both options now, a sensible move for a family heading to Japan is ordering a cheap backup pair from Zenni before the trip, specifically as insurance against a kid losing or breaking their only pair somewhere between flights. <a href="https://zenni.pxf.io/m4Zy0a">Zenni's site</a> is worth a look if that's your situation. If that backup pair also goes missing, or if glasses weren't part of the plan at all, the walk-in chains are still sitting in nearly every train station shopping complex in the country, free exam included, no reservation required.

## Was it worth comparing

Yes, mostly because the two options solve different problems rather than competing on the same one. Zenni is cheaper on a per-dollar basis if you plan ahead and already have a prescription. The walk-in chains cost a little more but ask nothing of you in advance, which matters more than a few dollars when the actual situation is a kid without glasses on day three of a two-week trip.
