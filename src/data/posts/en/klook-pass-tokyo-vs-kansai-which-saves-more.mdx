The Tokyo pass's 2-attraction entry tier runs about $46.25, and booking Skytree plus Tokyo Tower separately costs roughly the same or less at current prices, no real edge there. The Kansai pass's 3-attraction entry tier runs about $35, against roughly $37.50 to book teamLab Botanical Garden Osaka, HARUKAS 300, and the Umeda Sky Building separately, a modest real win, a few dollars, but a win.

## The mechanics aren't identical either

Price isn't the only real difference. The Tokyo pass lets you pick 2 to 7 attractions and gives you a 90 day window after your first redemption to use the rest, generous enough to spread across a longer trip or even a return visit. The Kansai pass runs a narrower 3 to 8 attraction range and a tighter 30 day window, built more for a single concentrated Osaka stay than a trip you might come back to months later. If your Japan trip is split across multiple visits over a year, that redemption window matters as much as the yen total, and it's the one place Tokyo's pass has a real structural edge over Kansai's regardless of which attractions you pick.

## Where the real savings shows up

Both passes follow the identical pattern once you add one specific kind of attraction: something expensive. On the Tokyo side, that's Disney or the Warner Bros. Studio Tour. On the Kansai side, it's Universal Studios Japan, where a single adult ticket alone runs 8,900 to 11,900 yen, several times the price of any standard-tier attraction on either list. Add one of those premium options to either pass, and the savings stop being a rounding error.

## Was it worth buying either one

Depends entirely on your itinerary, not the city. Sticking to standard attractions only, Kansai's pass edges out Tokyo's, a small real discount versus roughly breaking even. But the real decision point for either pass is whether Disney, Warner Bros., or USJ is already on your list, since that's what turns either pass from a marginal deal into a genuine one. If your trip covers both cities and includes one of those premium attractions in each, buying both passes separately makes more sense than trying to force everything onto one, the standard-tier attractions aren't transferable between the two lists anyway.
