Every guidebook list of "free things to do in Tokyo" runs the same handful of spots: a shrine, a park, an observation deck. What almost never makes the list is the thing that actually saved us on a long stay: the small neighborhood playground down whatever street you happen to be staying on. Japan has them everywhere, they cost nothing, and they're some of the best jet lag medicine there is for kids who've been cooped up on trains and in temples all day.

## Where we found this one

We spent an extended stretch in Katsushika, a residential ward in Tokyo that isn't on most tourist itineraries at all, and a five minute walk from where we were staying was a playground built into the space under an elevated train line. Swings, a slide, open ground to run around on, no fence, no gate, no attendant, nothing to pay. Slightly odd real estate, tucked under the tracks, but it's a genuinely common setup in Japanese cities: the space under an elevated line is otherwise dead land, so a lot of it ends up as small public parks and playgrounds instead.

## Why this matters more than it sounds like it should

A day of temples, train transfers, and standing in line for something is exhausting for kids in a way that doesn't show up until it suddenly does. A free playground within walking distance of wherever you're staying is a release valve you don't have to plan around or pay for, no ticket, no reservation, no closing time to work backward from. We didn't go looking for this one, we just walked out the door one afternoon and found it, and that's true of most neighborhoods in Japan, not just ours.

## If you want to find one

There's no app or map for this, and that's kind of the point. Ask wherever you're staying if there's a nearby park with a playground, or just look on a map for a green patch near your accommodation, small ones often won't have an official name or listing. If you're near an elevated train line, check underneath it specifically, that's where a lot of the smaller ones hide. While you're out, a conbini snack or drink run afterward is its own small, cheap reward, most bottled drinks and juices run somewhere in the 99 to 199 yen range, worth having a little cash or a Suica loaded up for the walk back.

The whole outing costs nothing but the time it takes to walk there, which, on a trip where everything else has a price tag, is worth remembering exists.
