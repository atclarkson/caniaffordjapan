If the only Round1 content you've seen on this site is [Sakai pricing](/blog/round1-bowling-arcade-cost-japan-family/) and [a vending machine that hands out bowling shoes for Harper's birthday](/blog/round1-bowling-shoe-vending-machine-harper-birthday/), it would be reasonable to assume the chain is an Osaka thing. It isn't. We found one in Narashino, Chiba, the day after a full day at Tokyo Disney Sea, and it's the same chain, same pricing structure, just nowhere near Osaka.

## Where this one is

This location, Round1 Stadium Narashino, sits in Narashino, Chiba, close enough to the Tokyo Disney Resort area that it's a realistic add-on for a family already staying out that way rather than downtown Tokyo proper. The nearest station is JR Keiyo Line's Makuhari-Toyosuna, about a 6-minute walk, and the store also runs a free shuttle from JR Tsudanuma, Keisei Tsudanuma, and Kaihin-Makuhari stations, worth knowing if you're not driving. Free parking covers around 260 cars for anyone who is.

## What's there

Same format as the Sakai location we've already covered: bowling, karaoke rooms, an arcade floor, and the Spocha sports-and-amusement pass that covers most of the building on a time block instead of paying per activity. This specific store also runs a Kids Spocha tier for kids up to third grade, a real detail worth knowing for a family with younger kids who don't want the full adult-pass price. For the real current bowling and Spocha pricing itself, it's the same nationwide structure we already broke down for Sakai, worth reading there rather than us re-deriving numbers we didn't personally pay at this specific store on this visit.

## Why this one, this trip

This was the day after Tokyo Disney Sea, not planned months in advance, just the kind of stop that happens when three kids have energy left over and a Round1 happens to be nearby. We didn't take a receipt photo or a scoreboard shot this time, just the one picture of the girls at the door, so this is a shorter entry than our Sakai posts, a real visit without the full paper trail. The honest takeaway for anyone planning a Tokyo-area trip: check for a Round1 near wherever you're staying before assuming you'd need to detour to Osaka for one, the chain has locations across Japan, this is just the one we happened to be near.
