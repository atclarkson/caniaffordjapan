No name on the receipt, no name we wrote down for the restaurant itself, just a casual pizza spot in Asakusa on a Tuesday night that happened to have a craft IPA on the menu. The bottle is the whole story here: a green label, hop artwork, four small stars, and "55 IBU" printed near the bottom.

## The bottle itself

That 55 IBU figure, a measure of bitterness, matches Goose Island's IPA exactly, a Chicago-born beer most familiar from grocery store shelves in the US, not a Japanese neighborhood pizza counter. The label's circular hop-and-star mark matches Goose Island's current bottle design exactly. Mainstream Japanese lager, Asahi, Kirin, Sapporo, is the default at most restaurants here, so finding an import IPA on a random menu wasn't the expected outcome of ordering a round of beers with dinner.

## How it got there

Goose Island isn't a random find, it has a documented Japan presence going back to 2016, when the brand first brought a selection of its beers into the Japanese market. Since then, Goose Island has run restaurant tie-ins in Japan, including a promotion pairing Goose IPA with gourmet burgers at four well-known Japanese burger spots. None of that confirms this specific pizza counter stocks it regularly, restaurant menus change, but it explains why an American craft IPA can turn up somewhere that isn't a dedicated craft beer bar. The branding abroad is simplified too: the US label reads "Goose Island IPA," while Japanese promotional material for the same beer shortens it to "Goose IPA," a small naming difference worth knowing if you're searching a menu for the full name and coming up empty.

## Why this isn't the norm

Craft beer in Japan skews toward dedicated bars rather than restaurant menus, and it costs more when you find it: a full pint at a craft-focused bar commonly runs ¥1,000 to ¥1,300 in Tokyo, well above a standard lager at the same kind of casual spot. A convenience store shelf is the more reliable place to find a Japanese-brewed craft IPA like Yoho Brewing's Aooni; a restaurant pouring an imported one by the bottle is the less common version of the same story.

## Worth remembering

Nothing about this moment was planned or researched in advance, it's the kind of detail that only shows up by ordering a round of drinks rather than assuming the menu. If an unfamiliar bottle turns up on a Japan trip, the label usually has the answer before a search does.
