The photo above is the real payment screen from an October 2023 dinner in Asakusa: 6,840 yen total, split five ways, 1,368 yen a head. That's about $9 per person for a full sit-down dinner for our family of five, drinks included. It's not a one-off deal or a lucky off-menu find, it's just what Torikizoku costs.

## What the bill breaks down to

- **Total: 6,840 yen (roughly $45).**
- **Per person: 1,368 yen (roughly $9).**
- **Party size: 5, two adults and three kids.**
- **What's driving that number:** a flat per-item price across the entire menu, not a discount or a kids' menu trick.

## Why the math works out this clean

Torikizoku prices its whole menu at one flat rate, 390 yen a plate as of this year, chicken skewers, rice sets, sides, and drinks alike. Order five rounds or fifteen, the per-item math never changes, which is exactly why a family bill lands on a tidy number like this instead of the usual pile of mismatched entree and drink prices. Reported per-item pricing does vary a little by branch and year, from around 280 yen at some locations up to 390 yen at others, so treat that number as a solid ballpark rather than a guarantee at every storefront.

## How ordering works

Tables order from a touchscreen tablet, with an English option at most branches, so there's no language barrier to plan around. Some locations have you grab a queue number from a tablet near the entrance first. All-you-can-drink is available at many branches and draws a real crowd for it, but it usually comes with a roughly two-hour limit during busy periods, worth knowing before you plan a long, leisurely family dinner around it. A kamameshi rice pot, if you order one, gets cooked tableside partway through the meal rather than arriving with everything else, so put that order in early if you want it before dessert territory.

## The chain behind the receipt

Torikizoku started in 1985 as a single shop and has grown into one of Japan's biggest yakitori chains, over 600 locations across the country and more than 700 worldwide once you count its newer international branches (Singapore's first outlet opened in 2026, alcohol priced separately from the flat food rate there). None of that history shows up on the bill, but it's part of why the flat pricing holds up everywhere: it's a company-wide model, not a one-location promotion.

## Was it worth it

For a family of five eating well for about $45 total, obviously yes, and it's the kind of place worth defaulting to on a trip rather than saving for a special night. The appeal isn't a secret deal or a tourist discount, it's a plainly simple pricing model that happens to favor a group that orders a lot of small things, which describes most families traveling with kids. Order too little and you've still spent almost nothing; order too much and the worst case is a few extra 390 yen plates, not a bill that spirals the way an a la carte menu's drinks and sides tend to once a family of five starts ordering freely.
