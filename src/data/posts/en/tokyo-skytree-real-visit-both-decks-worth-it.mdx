The question wasn't whether to go up Tokyo Skytree, we'd already decided that before we left our apartment that morning in March 2023. The real question was whether to stop at the first deck or pay extra to climb higher into the second one, and that's the part we didn't settle until we were standing at the ticket counter deciding in person.

## Getting there was the easy part

We were staying in Asakusa that stretch of the trip, and Skytree is about as low-effort a day trip as Tokyo offers from there: Tokyo Skytree Station, on the Tobu line, is one stop and roughly two minutes from Asakusa Station, with the tower's entrance basically attached to the platform. No transfer, no real navigation required, just a short ride and we were standing under the thing.

## Picking up the tickets in person

We bought our way in at the counter rather than online that day, no advance booking, just walking up and deciding what we wanted once we were already there. That's not how we'd recommend doing it now, Skytree's own pricing has since moved to a dynamic system where booking ahead online saves real money over a same-day counter ticket, something we've [written about separately](/blog/tokyo-skytree-tembo-deck-ticket-price-cost/) with current figures. In March 2023 we didn't have that pressure yet, pricing was simpler, and the real decision in front of us was just floor 350 alone, or pay more to keep going up.

## Floor 350: the first deck

The elevator ride up alone is part of the experience, fast enough that your ears notice it, and floor 350 delivers exactly what the name promises: a flat, wraparound view of Tokyo from height, the kind of scale that's hard to process standing at street level. It would have been a complete visit on its own. We didn't stop there.

## Tembo Galleria: the second climb

A separate elevator took us up to the Tembo Galleria at 450 meters, and what stuck with us wasn't just the extra hundred meters of height, it was the structure itself, a spiraling lattice walkway wrapped around the tower's upper section, not a flat observation floor like the one below it. Looking straight down through the lattice from that high up is a different sensation than looking out a window, the kind of moment that's hard to describe secondhand and a real reason the second deck earns a separate name rather than just being "upstairs" from the first. It's a short loop, the Galleria doesn't take long to walk end to end, but it's the one part of the day none of us stopped talking about on the elevator back down.

## The cafe, and a view that kept giving

We stopped at the Skytree Cafe partway through, a small but real pause built into the visit rather than a rushed up-and-down. The cafe on floor 350 is known for its Skytree Soft, a soft-serve topped with a tower-shaped cookie, around 500 yen per the cafe's own menu, the kind of small splurge that fits naturally into a day already built around looking at the view rather than rushing through it. The view didn't stop once we left the tower either. From our own apartment window in the days around this visit, Skytree was close enough to watch light up at night without going anywhere, a detail that made the whole stretch of this stay feel less like sightseeing and more like living somewhere with a landmark next door, the tower turning into a nightly backdrop rather than a one-time stop on an itinerary.

## What else was happening that week

This Skytree day sat inside a packed stretch of our March 2023 Tokyo stay. Early cherry blossoms were just starting to open around the city that same week, weeks before the main season we've since [written up separately](/blog/cherry-blossoms-vs-hydrangeas-free-flower-seasons-japan/), a handful of trees ahead of the rest of the city rather than a full bloom yet. We also had a real conversation on camera about [which transit card makes sense](/blog/suica-pasmo-icoca-ic-card-japan/) for a family of five, the kind of practical logistics talk that doesn't make the highlight reel but shaped how we got around for the rest of the stay. Around the same stretch of days we [tried tonkatsu for the first time](/blog/tonkatsu-first-time-family-cost-history/), three kids and two adults all figuring out the free cabbage refills together. None of that happened the same afternoon as Skytree specifically, but it's the same week, the same version of us, still working out this city one new thing at a time rather than arriving as experts.

## What it costs to do this now

We don't have the exact yen figure we paid at the counter that day, pricing structures have changed enough since 2023 that quoting it now would be more confusing than useful. What we do know is the current number: Skytree's combo ticket covering both floor 350 and the Tembo Galleria currently runs from around 3,000 yen booked online ahead of time, up to roughly 3,800 yen for a same-day counter ticket on a busy date, per the current pricing we've [broken down separately](/blog/tokyo-skytree-tembo-deck-ticket-price-cost/). Buying online in advance rather than walking up to the counter the way we did is the one piece of advice we'd pass along from this visit, it's the same tower and the same two decks, just a better way to pay for them now than the way we found out about in person.

## Was it worth the second climb

Yes, and specifically because of what the Tembo Galleria is rather than just its extra height. Floor 350 alone is a real, complete Skytree visit, flat, wide, impressive on its own, and plenty of visitors stop there without missing much. The Galleria's spiral lattice walkway is a structurally different experience, not just a taller version of the same view, and that difference is what justified the extra cost for us. If budget only stretches to one deck, floor 350 does the job. If it stretches further, the climb up into the lattice is the part that changes how the visit feels, not just how high you went.
