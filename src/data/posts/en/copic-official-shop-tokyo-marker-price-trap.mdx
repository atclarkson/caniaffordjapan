The trap: grabbing the cheapest Copic marker on the shelf without checking which color range it covers. Copic sells three separate product lines at three separate prices, and the cheapest one doesn't carry every color the more expensive ones do.

## The three tiers, and what each one costs

Japan's retailers raised Copic prices in February 2026, so current figures matter more than older guides floating around online. As of that increase, a single Copic Ciao marker runs 330 yen tax included, a Copic Sketch runs 495 yen, and a Copic Classic runs 638 yen. All three use the same alcohol-based, acid-free ink. The difference isn't quality, it's range and format, and that's exactly where the trap sits.

## Why the cheap one isn't just a smaller version of the expensive one

Ciao and Sketch share the same two nib types, a Super Brush tip and a Medium Broad tip, and both refill from the same ink bottles. The real catch is color count: Sketch comes in the full 358-color range, Ciao only in 180. Reach for the cheaper Ciao assuming it's simply a discount version of Sketch, and roughly half the color range doesn't exist in that line at all, not a stocking gap, a permanent limitation of the product itself. Classic is its own separate thing again, a dual-nib marker with a Broad tip on one end and a Fine tip on the other, aimed more at traditional illustration work than the blending-focused Sketch and Ciao lines.

## Refills change the real math

A full-size marker isn't the only way to keep a color in rotation. A single ink refill bottle restocks a Sketch marker up to 12 times and a Ciao up to 9, a real detail that changes the actual cost of staying stocked in a favorite color well below the price of replacing a whole marker every time it runs dry. For anyone building a serious collection rather than a one-off souvenir purchase, refills are the real long-term budget lever, not the sticker price on the marker itself.

## Buying it here instead of back home

Pricing in the US runs noticeably higher than Japan's current retail figures, a single Copic Sketch commonly lists around 6 to 8 US dollars at American art supply retailers, against the 495 yen (roughly 3 to 3.50 US dollars depending on the exchange rate) a Sketch marker costs straight off the shelf in Japan. That's one retailer's listing rather than a full market survey, so treat it as a rough comparison rather than a fixed ratio, but the direction is consistent with what other imported Japanese specialty goods tend to cost once shipping and import markup get added overseas. For anyone already planning to build out a collection, buying the bulk of it here rather than after getting home is a real, meaningful saving, not just a few cents a marker.

## Where to find one

We can't confirm a specific Tokyo address for an official Copic shop from research alone, sources consistently confirm one exists in Osaka's Umeda Chikagai underground mall, but Tokyo listings were thinner and less current. Too Corporation's own store locator is the reliable way to confirm a current Tokyo location and hours before planning a special trip around it, worth checking directly rather than trusting a single blog's address.

## The shop behind the shelf

Copic itself traces back to 1919, a Shibuya stationery shop called Izumiya Art Supply Store, founded by a 28-year-old named Soyo Ishii. The company didn't become Too Corporation until 1992, and the Copic marker line itself is newer still, launched in 1987 with 71 original colors, the name derived from "copies," built specifically not to dissolve the toner on a photocopy, a real functional reason behind the name rather than a stylistic choice. The retail side split off as a separate company, Tools Corporation, in 1999, then merged back with Too's marker division in 2015, which is why the storefront in our photo carries both names, TOOLS above the door and COPIC OFFICIAL SHOP on the glass below it.

## What to buy if color range matters

If the goal is matching a specific color scheme, exact color numbers from Copic's full chart, buying individual Sketch markers one at a time covers all 358 options, something no pre-packaged starter set guarantees since most sets ship with a curated subset rather than the complete range. Building a set marker by marker at the Official Shop, picking exact numbers off the wall rather than taking whatever a boxed set includes, is the real advantage of buying in person here over ordering a fixed set online before the trip.

## Was it worth the stop

For a family with an artist in it, completely. A storefront most visitors walk straight past turned into the real highlight of that afternoon for one very specific 8 year old, and the pricing behind it, three tiers, two of them sharing ink but not color range, refills cheaper than replacement, turned out to be a real, useful thing to know before buying anything off that wall.
