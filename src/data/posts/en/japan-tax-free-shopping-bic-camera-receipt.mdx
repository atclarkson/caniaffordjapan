We still have the receipt. December 2023, Bic Camera's store in front of Chiba Station, a Nintendo Switch bundle, a pastel Joy-Con controller, and a Mario game, five items, ¥50,998 total. The line on the receipt that matters most for this post isn't the total, it's the one just below it: 内、消費税等 ¥0, consumption tax included, zero yen. That's not a discount code or a sale, it's how Japan's tax-free shopping system worked for us that day, and it's worth understanding before your own trip, especially with a real change coming in November 2026.

## How it currently works

Japan charges a 10% consumption tax on most goods, and as of this writing, foreign visitors can have that 10% waived immediately at the register, not refunded later, at any store registered as a tax-free shop. Bic Camera, Yodobashi, and most large electronics and department store chains qualify. The requirements are simple: show your passport at checkout, and spend at least 5,000 yen pre-tax at that store on that day. Meet both, and the tax simply doesn't get added to your total in the first place.

## Reading our own receipt

Ours breaks down to a Nintendo Switch console, a pastel Joy-Con controller, and Mario software, five line items total, with a 2,000 yen in-store voucher applied on top before the total. Subtotal and total both land at ¥50,998, and the consumption tax line reads exactly ¥0. No separate refund form, no waiting, no airport counter, the discount was already baked into the number we paid at the register that afternoon.

## Stacking a real coupon on top

Bic Camera and its sister chain Kojima also run a standing tourist coupon, free, no purchase required to get it, just pull it up on your phone at checkout: an extra 7% off electronics, cameras, watches, and toys, 5% off cosmetics and daily goods, 3% off sake, calculated on top of the already tax-free price. It requires the same passport and 5,000 yen pre-tax minimum as the tax exemption itself, so if you're already qualifying for one, you qualify for the other. We didn't have this specific coupon in hand in 2023, our 2,000 yen discount came from a different in-store promotion, but it's worth pulling up before you check out today, it costs nothing to have ready.

## What changes November 1, 2026

This is the part worth planning around if your trip lands after that date: Japan is switching from this immediate at-register exemption to a pay-first, refund-later system. You'll pay the full tax-included price at checkout everywhere, then claim the consumption tax back at the airport before you leave, using either a dedicated refund counter or, at select airports, a phone-based process through Visit Japan Web. The refund has to be claimed within 90 days of the purchase date. A few of the old restrictions go away with the change too, the 500,000 yen daily purchase cap, the requirement that consumables leave the store in sealed tamper-evident bags, and the rule keeping general goods and consumables on separate receipts all get dropped under the new system.

Under the new system, you'll also need to present the actual purchased goods for customs inspection at the airport before departure, not just the receipts, so pack tax-free items somewhere accessible rather than buried at the bottom of a checked bag. The initial paperwork can be done ahead of time through Visit Japan Web at several major international airports, including both of Tokyo's, Narita and Haneda, along with Kansai, Chubu, Fukuoka, New Chitose, and Naha, letting you handle part of the process on your phone instead of standing in a dedicated terminal line.

## What this means for your trip

If you're traveling before November 2026, expect the same experience we had, passport out, tax off the total on the spot, receipt in hand as proof like ours. After that date, budget to pay full sticker price at the register, keep every tax-free item reachable in your carry-on rather than checked luggage, and build in real time at the airport to file for the refund before your flight, not after you've already gone through security with nothing left to spare. Either way, the passport and the 5,000 yen threshold are the two things worth remembering regardless of which side of the change your trip falls on.

## Was it worth understanding

Completely, and it cost us nothing to learn beyond reading our own receipt closely enough to notice the zero. A Nintendo Switch bundle for just under 51,000 yen with the tax already stripped out is real money saved on a single purchase, and that's before stacking a free store coupon most tourists never think to pull up. Whichever system is running when you land, walking in already knowing the threshold and the paperwork puts you ahead of most people in that checkout line.
