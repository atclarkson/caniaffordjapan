## Kurumaya Ramen, Urayasu

Kurumaya is a real ramen chain with locations across the Kanto region, not a one-off local shop, and the Urayasu branch sits at 3 Chome-5-45 Fujimi, a short distance from Tokyo Disney Resort rather than inside it. Per the restaurant's own current Tabelog listing, chilled soy sauce noodles run 1,000 yen, gyoza is 290 yen, and draft beer is 680 yen, with a half bowl of rice thrown in free. There's also a real lunch deal worth knowing: order between 11:00am and 3:00pm with any ramen, and the gyoza price drops from 262 yen to 157 yen. The shop stays open late, 11:00am to 1:00am most days, last orders at 12:45am, useful if a Disney day runs long and dinner ends up happening well after dark. Closed Tuesdays.

Other Kurumaya branches we checked list slightly different numbers, a Fukiage location prices its miso ramen at 800 yen and gyoza at 400 yen, which tracks with how chain ramen shops in Japan often price each location a little differently rather than running one fixed national menu. Worth a quick check against whichever branch is nearby rather than assuming the Urayasu numbers travel to every location.

For two adults each ordering a bowl of chilled soy sauce noodles with a shared plate of gyoza and one beer to split, that's 1,000 plus 1,000 plus 290 plus 680, 2,970 yen before tax for the adults alone. Kids' pricing isn't listed on the restaurant's own page, so we're not guessing a number for that part, a real gap in what's publicly available rather than something to round over.

## Eating inside the parks instead

Tokyo Disney Resort's own counter-service spots land in a surprisingly similar range. Casbah Food Court in DisneySea currently lists Chicken Curry with Rice at 950 yen and Beef Curry with Rice at 1,200 yen, and China Voyager serves a ramen option of its own on its menu, though exact current pricing for that specific dish is harder to pin down than the curry figures. Across the resort's quick-service spots generally, a typical counter meal runs somewhere around 900 to 1,800 yen per person, not far from what a bowl of noodles and a plate of gyoza costs at Kurumaya. Table-service restaurants inside the parks run considerably higher, anywhere from roughly 2,500 to 9,000 yen depending on the spot, before drinks, a tier Kurumaya isn't competing with at all.

## The real comparison

This isn't the dramatic "half the price outside the park" gap we found with some Klook-bookable attractions, counter-service curry and a Kurumaya ramen dinner land in roughly the same yen range. What tips the decision is logistics, not price. Eating inside the park saves a resort re-entry and the walk back from wherever you're staying, worth something real on a day already spent standing in lines. Eating at Kurumaya makes more sense on an arrival night before a park day even starts, or after a long day when the parks' own restaurants are either closed or not worth the wait, and a late-night kitchen open past midnight is a real advantage the parks themselves don't offer. Neither option is the "smart" one and the other the "wasteful" one here, they solve different parts of the same trip.

## Worth knowing about

If a Tokyo Disney Resort stay already has you in Urayasu for a night before or after park days, a real, specific, family-friendly ramen shop a short walk away is worth having on the list, especially with a kitchen that stays open past midnight.
