A full week of the Worldschool Pop-Up Hub's Tokyo stop in October 2023 cost us zero yen to join, the same no-admission setup [we later wrote up for a single Kawasaki meetup day](/blog/worldschool-popup-hub-kawasaki-day-cost/), just spread across several real days this time instead of one. This was our actual first experience with the Pop-Up Hub, months before Kawasaki, and a full week looks different from a single afternoon in ways worth laying out separately.

## What a popup week is

We've already covered the Pop-Up Hub itself in detail: a real, informal community of full-time traveling families, run by the Carlsons, no fee at the door, no booking link, just families who happen to be in the same city finding each other. A single meetup day, like the Kawasaki one, is one gathering, one park, one afternoon. A popup week is the same idea stretched across several days in a row, a different free park or meeting spot each day, families drifting in and out depending on their own travel schedules rather than everyone committing to the whole week at once.

## Two real days, two different free parks

October 28 took us to Heiwanomorikoen in Ota, a playground built around a real rope-climb structure worth the detour on its own, the kind of free park we've separately written up for its obstacle-course value. That day's gathering was smaller, just the kids finding the ropes and nets while the adults compared notes on where everyone had been and where they were headed next, the real substance of a popup day having nothing to do with a planned activity.

October 29 moved to a different park in Taito, a concrete slide structure that turned into the group photo above, roughly ten kids from however many families had shown up that particular day. No roster, no sign-up sheet, the loose structure is the actual point: whoever's around that day is the group for that day.

## What it cost, and what it didn't

The meetups themselves: free, every single day, the same as the Kawasaki day we've already priced out in full. What we did spend money on that week was ordinary trip overhead unrelated to the popup itself, meals, transit around Tokyo, nothing the Pop-Up Hub charged for or required. The real cost of a full popup week isn't a fee, it's the same thing we noted about the single Kawasaki day: the time spent finding out a popup is happening in a city you're already passing through, not money changing hands once you're there.

## How you'd even find out this was happening

Nobody hands out a schedule for a Pop-Up Hub week the way a theme park hands out a map. We found out about this one the same informal, word-of-mouth way other full-time traveling families track pop-ups city to city, other worldschoolers mentioning it, not an ad or a booking page. That's the real barrier to entry, not a fee: a family has to already be plugged into that loose network, or stumble into someone who is, before a free week like this one shows up on their radar at all. Worth knowing going in if a community like this appeals to you, since no amount of googling "worldschool popup Tokyo" turns up a signup form the way a paid tour would.

## Was it worth a whole week

Completely, and more so than a single day would have been. One meetup gives a family one afternoon of connection; a week gave our kids several different days with several different kid combinations, and gave us several different conversations with other full-time-traveling parents rather than one rushed exchange before everyone scattered. If a Pop-Up Hub week lines up with a city you're already in, the math is about as easy as it gets: completely free, and the only real cost is making sure you know it's happening.
