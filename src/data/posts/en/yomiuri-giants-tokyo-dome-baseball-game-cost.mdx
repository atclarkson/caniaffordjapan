We took Lily and Harper to a Yomiuri Giants game at Tokyo Dome on a late summer evening, and if you asked either of them what stood out, it wasn't a home run. It was the vendors. Japanese baseball stadiums run on a whole service culture American ballparks don't have, staff walking the aisles with kegs strapped to their backs pouring beer table-side, so the girls spent more time watching the snack economy than the actual game. Either way, it's a genuinely different, genuinely affordable night out, and the seat you pick changes the price a lot.

## What a seat actually costs

Tokyo Dome prices Giants tickets by seat category and by how in-demand the specific date is (Category I is standard, Category II covers most games, Category S is Saturdays):

| Seat | Category I | Category II | Category S (Saturdays) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outfield standing | 1,500 yen | 1,800 yen | not sold |
| Reserved C | 2,200 yen | 2,500 yen | 2,800 yen |
| Reserved C, student rate | 1,000 yen | 1,000 yen | 1,000 yen |
| Reserved B | 4,500 yen | 4,900 yen | 5,300 yen |
| Reserved A | 5,800 yen | 6,200 yen | 6,600 yen |
| Reserved S | 6,800 yen | 7,300 yen | 7,700 yen |

There's a wide gap between the cheap seats and the premium ones (a few tiers above Reserved S run well past 10,000 yen, private box territory, not what most families need), but even the standard Reserved C seats are genuinely affordable for a professional sports game, and outfield standing tickets are cheaper than a movie ticket back home.

## Was it worth it

For the price of a couple of the cheaper reserved seats, an evening at Tokyo Dome beat most of the paid attractions we've covered on this site, no timed entry, no line, just a stadium full of genuinely into-it fans and enough vendor traffic that the girls never had a reason to complain about being bored. If a baseball game wasn't already on your Tokyo list, it probably should be, it's one of the cheaper nights out we've had here, and the kids still bring it up.
